# Part A — the report

## FRUS TEI: missing documents in 1952–1954, vol. IX, Part 1, and six classes of encoding defect

[FRUS Explorer](https://github.com/joshbotts/FRUS-Explorer) is an independent reader for *Foreign Relations of the United States*. It parses the TEI you publish in `HistoryAtState/frus`. This report lists what a scan of all 744 files in `volumes/` found, in the hope that it saves you time.

**Revision.** Every line number and byte offset is relative to commit `550a8c5c5` (2026-09-15). `volumes/` is unchanged from there to `master` at `8e5da08c1` (2026-09-25): `git diff 550a8c5c5 8e5da08c1 -- volumes` is empty.

**None of this is visible to validation.** All 744 files are well-formed XML. A closing tag in the wrong place, a page break with the wrong number and a date attribute that contradicts its own text all pass a schema, which is presumably why they have gone unnoticed.

**How sure each row is.** The structure, pagination, cross-reference, date and header rows each carry a mark:

- **confirmed** — the file itself settles it: its own contents list, an image that exists, or text that contradicts an attribute. For structure, the correction was also applied to a copy of the file, which was re-parsed and checked for the nesting stated.
- **question** — the file points one way but only the printed volume or the page image can settle it.
- **not a defect** — eight cross-references, listed so that you need not rediscover them.

The transcription rows carry no mark. Each quotes the text as the file reads it, and the printed page decides.

Please tell us when a row is wrong. Each one says what was checked, so the deliberate ones should be quick to dismiss.

### 1. `frus1952-54v09p1`: Documents 900–946 are missing

*Foreign Relations*, 1952–1954, vol. IX, Part 1 ends too early. The last 47 documents of its chapter on the Arab states and Israel (`ch4`), October to December 1954, are not in the file and not on history.state.gov.

**What the file holds now**

- The last document is `d899` (No. 899, Caffery to the Department, 28 September 1954), at line 113036. The last page break is `pg_1660`, at line 113010.
- The errata section follows directly, at line 113082.
- Part 2 (`frus1952-54v09p2`) begins at `pg_1743` (line 10591) with `d947` (line 10612).

**Why this is a loss and not the book's shape**

- Part 1's own contents list links Part 2 at `frus1952-54v09p2#pg_1743` (line 13897), so the pagination is continuous and pp. 1661–1742 belong to Part 1.
- The combined index in Part 2 cites those pages 352 times: 352 of the 652 broken references in the whole corpus, to 71 distinct pages, from `pg_1661` to `pg_1741`.
- `frus1955-57v14` (line 9356) cites "*Foreign Relations*, 1952–1954, volume IX, Part 1, pages 1660–1741 *passim*" for the *Bat Galim* case, which opens in d899.
- On history.state.gov, `frus1952-54v09p1/d899` answers 200, and `/d900`, `/d946` and `/pg_1661` answer 404 (checked 2026-10-01).

**Where the text is**

Your own history has it. Commit `d7c15c90c` (2016-10-31, "Recent legacy review volume delivery by vendor") replaced the file. Its parent holds 946 documents and ends at `pg_1741`; the delivered file holds 899 and ends at `pg_1660`.

`missing-documents.csv` lists the 47 documents as they stand in `d7c15c90c^`: number, heading, date, the page each begins on, and its line in that revision. The dates run from 1 October to 31 December 1954; two of the 47 carry none (`d914`, and `d939`, an Editorial Note).

**Correction.** Restore Documents 900–946 and page breaks 1661–1741 from `d7c15c90c^`, after `d899` and inside the same chapter. The 352 index references then resolve as they stand. Page 1742 has no break in either revision; it is presumably the blank verso before Part 2.

The scan compares the last page of each part with the first page of the next. This is the only gap among the 22 pairs of consecutive parts that share one pagination.

### 5. Dates

`dates.csv` has 205 rows in 89 volumes.

**5.1 The encoded year contradicts the printed year — 172 documents in 69 volumes.** The `<date>` of a document's dateline carries a `@when` whose year is not the year printed inside the same element. For example, `frus1945Malta` d273 prints "November 27, 1944—7 p. m." and carries `when="1945-11-27T19:00:00+03:00"`; its file number, 740.00114 EW/11–2744, agrees with the text.

One of the two is wrong in each, unless the book reproduces a slip in the original. Sometimes it is plainly the text (`frus1914Supp` d483 prints "September 28, 2014"). Often it is plainly the attribute: 48 of them are in frus1891, a volume that prints correspondence of late 1890 and early 1892, and every one of the 48 is encoded 1891. `frus1875v02` has 11, `frus1914` 10, `frus1914-20v01` 7.

The CSV gives the attribute and the text for each. Where the attribute is wrong, `frus:doc-dateTime-min` and `-max` on the document are wrong with it, and so is the range of any division it stretches.

**5.2 The document's year contradicts its own file number — 13 more documents.** The encoded date is outside the volume's years, while the document's decimal file number carries the same month and day in a year the volume covers.

In 12 of them the text and the attribute agree with each other, so these are questions for the page image:

- `frus1955-57v15` d334 is dated "May 4, 1965"; its file is 684A.86/5–456.
- `frus1955-57v12` d395 is dated "March 19, 1951"; its file is 888.2553/3–1957. Because of this one document, every front-matter division of the volume carries `frus:doc-dateTime-min="1951-03-19…"`.
- `frus1945v03` d168 is dated "April 3, 1944"; its file is 740.00119 E.W./4–345.

In 1 the dateline prints no year, so there is no page to check. `frus1947v06` d425 (line 44364) prints "undated", and its `when="1948-10-09"` is your own inference (`ana="#date_undated-inferred-from-document-content-and-sibling-dates"`). Its file number is 740.0011 PW (Peace)/10–947, and its footnote says the paper was "Presented to Mr. Borton by Mr. Graves on October 9". The volume is 1947's, so the inferred year is probably 1947.

**5.3 Ranges that end before they begin — 1 document and 19 divisions.** `frus:doc-dateTime-min` is later than `frus:doc-dateTime-max`.

- The document is `frus1969-76v27` d25 (line 14476). Its `<date>` reads `from="1973-07-24T10:43:00-05:00" to="1973-07-24T12:35:00-03:00"`. The `-03:00` puts the end at 10:35 Eastern, before the start. `to="…T12:35:00-05:00"` is probably meant.
- The 19 divisions are in 13 volumes: 10 subchapters, 6 chapters, 2 compilations and 1 section. For example, `frus1941v03` `ch1subch1` has min 22 December 1941 and max 14 January 1941. They are computed values and need recomputing from the documents they hold.

### 6. Transcription

These are small, and most are in source notes. `transcription.csv` has 169 rows. Each quotes the text as the file reads it, with a line wrap or any other run of whitespace as one space. Nothing is added where an inline tag stands (`<hi>`, `<gloss>`, `<persName>`). A line break (`<lb/>`), like any other tag that is not inline, reads as a space whether or not the file has one there.

**6.1 A space lost before an inline tag — 1,213 sites in 40 files.** A comma is followed directly by `<gloss>`, so the text reads "Department of State,NEA/IAI Files" and "Lot 63 D 351,NSC". `frus1955-57v14` has 221 and `frus1964-68v15` has 140. The same file, `frus1955-57v14`, has 263 commas glued to `<persName>`. A semicolon glued to `<gloss>` occurs 327 times in 57 files. `transcription-glued-tags.csv` gives the counts for each of the 77 files, so a scripted pass can restore the spaces.

The same loss occurs with no tag involved: 5 source notes in 2 volumes read "Central Files1967-69" or "Central Files1970-73" (`frus1969-76ve01` d151, d153, d165 and d167; `frus1969-76ve04` d293).

**6.2 A closing parenthesis that closes nothing — 16 notes in 14 volumes.** The scan reads the notes that hold `))` and walks each one's parentheses in order. The markers of a list are set aside ("1) … 2) … 3)", "a) … b)", "i) … ii)": a number of one or two digits, a single letter or a short Roman numeral before a `)` that has no `(`), and so is a `))` that closes two real parentheses. What is left is a `)` with no `(` to match: either one `)` too many, or an opening one lost. The CSV quotes the text up to that `)`.

- One came in with commit `4e9694796` (2026-09-11): `frus1981-88v16` d395, footnote 4 (line 7084), "(Department of State, Central Foreign Policy File, Electronic Telegrams, D880704–0606))".
- In `frus1961-63v11` d142 (source note, line 27869) the `)` in question is not the doubled one: "Source: Department of State, Central Files, 737.56361/11-562)".

**6.3 Source notes in `frus1961-63v03` and `frus1961-63v04`.** Two patterns are concentrated there:

- 85 source notes have no full stop between the file designation and the classification: "Central Files, DEF 19 S VIET Secret. Drafted by…". 83 of the 85 are in these two volumes.
- 13 source notes read "S VIEI" for "S VIET": "POL 26-1 S VIEI Secret; Priority". The misreading stands 1 more time outside the source notes, in `frus1961-63v03`'s Sources section (line 6356), where the list explains the file: "SOC 14-1 S VIEI".

**6.4 Others.** 10 source notes have a doubled full stop, three of them "IRAN-U.S.." in `frus1964-68v22`. 6 have no space between the full stop and the classification ("US/A/M(SR)/1—.Confidential" in `frus1955-57v11`). 2 misspell the label: "Centrals Files" (`frus1955-57v16`) and "Central piles" (`frus1955-57v24`). And one heading, met while reading section 1's volume: the chapter that lost its documents is headed "…the Hashe–Mite Kingdom of Jordan" (`frus1952-54v09p1`, line 67638).

**6.5 "Department of State" misspelt in a dateline — 30 datelines in 22 volumes.** The scan reads the volumes before 1906. "Department op State", "Departmrnt of State", "Department of Stats", "Dapartment of State". They matter more than most misreadings because the dateline says who wrote the document. A dateline whose small capitals split the words (`D<hi rend="smallcaps">epartment of</hi> S<hi rend="smallcaps">tate</hi>`) reads "Department of State" and is not among them.

Some of these datelines break the line after the name with no space in the file: `State</hi>,<lb/><hi …>Washington`. The CSV quotes the `<lb/>` as a space, "State, Washington". So `frus1902` d267 (line 42149) is quoted "Department of Stat Washington", where the file has `Stat</hi><lb/>`.

### What was checked and is not reported

These were looked at and are not defects. They are listed so that nobody reports them again.
