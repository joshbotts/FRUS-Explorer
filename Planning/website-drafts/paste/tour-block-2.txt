          <div class="ebody">
            <p>The <b class="ui">Cite</b> tile copies a document's citation in History at State style, or in Chicago or Turabian style. It can also format it as BibTeX or RIS. <b class="ui">Share</b> sends the document to your Zotero library with your tags and notes, or sends the citation and its history.state.gov link to anyone.</p>
            <p class="prov"><span class="pk ed">From the editors</span><span>Each citation names the volume's editors and the document's number, and links to the page where the Office publishes its authoritative form.</span></p>

            <p class="where">Collections · iPad</p>
            <h3><span class="n">16.</span>Build your own curated document collections</h3>
          </div>
          <div class="ebody">
            <p>Create your own document collections from the documents you select. Use presets such as <b class="ui">Teaching reader</b> and <b class="ui">Briefing packet</b> to configure what information to include for each document in one step, or customize settings for each document. Add section headings or your own notes to curate your selections and share your interpretive findings. Incorporate document excerpts, a bibliography, a timeline, a list of people, or other apparatus elements that the app can generate directly from the documents.</p>
            <p>Export to PDF, Word or HTML. You can also share a native <code>.fruscollection</code> file with other FRUS Explorer users so they can build off your collection. Your research notes stay out of exports unless you add them.</p>

        <p class="sc">Part V</p>
        <h2 id="p5">Standing behind your work</h2>
        <p class="intro">FRUS allows researchers to link findings and interpretive judgments about the history of U.S. foreign policy to an evidentiary base of high-value documents widely accessible to all. FRUS Explorer facilitates this opportunity for reproducibility and verification of results.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="entries">
      <article class="entry side" id="e-method">
        <div class="etext">
          <p class="where">History and Data &amp; Recovery · iPhone, iPad and Mac</p>
          <h3><span class="n">18.</span>Show how you got your results</h3>
          <div class="ebody">
            <p>A claim against the record, such as “mentions of ‘United Nations’ fell after 1950,” has to include a scope of investigation. The app keeps that record as you work. Saving a working corpus fixes a set of documents, so counts made inside it mean the same thing next month, no matter what you download or gets published later. Chart a term's occurrence as a share of all documents, not just a raw count. Nearly every chart exports a spreadsheet that begins with an explanation of its method.</p>
            <p><b class="ui">History</b> lists every document you opened, every search you ran, and every collection you exported, filed by project. The method appendix turns your searches into a table for a detailed methods note: when each one ran, what it found, how many volumes were indexed, and what it searched within.</p>
            <p class="limit">The appendix keeps the searches that found nothing, because “I found nothing” is evidence only when it is anchored to a date and a scope. A count that hit the app's limit is written as a floor, such as “at least 1,000,” never as a total.</p>
            <p class="prov"><span class="pk cp">Made by the app</span><span>The appendix says what each count was drawn from: the volumes' text, or the app's model for a search by meaning.</span></p>
          </div>
        </div>

          <p class="where">Search ▸ ••• ▸ Find by citation · iPhone, iPad and Mac</p>
          <h3><span class="n">19.</span>Check a citation</h3>
          <div class="ebody">
            <p>Paste a citation from someone else's footnote, and the app opens the document it names, even in a volume you haven't browsed. It reads many of the forms historians write: a Chicago footnote, a short form such as <i>FRUS 1955–57, vol. XIV, doc. 23</i>, a history.state.gov link, or a citation that gives only a page.</p>
            <p><b class="ui">Batch</b> checks a whole chapter's footnotes at once. Each note gets a row that says it was resolved, how many documents it could be, or that nothing matched. A student's FRUS footnote either leads to a real document or it doesn't, so Batch gives teachers an easy way to check these references.</p>
            <p><b class="ui">Look up an abbreviation</b> searches the lists of abbreviations in every volume you've indexed. It shows each meaning with the number of volumes that use it.</p>
            <p class="limit">A page number alone can't say which of several short documents a citation means, so the app lists them all and picks none. Lookup covers the volumes in the app's catalog, not volumes you've added yourself from outside the app's download process.</p>
            <p class="prov"><span class="pk ed">From the editors</span><span>Lookup reads the volume titles, document numbers and page breaks of the Office's editions. In the pre-1955 volumes, document numbers were added to the digital editions, and the app says so when it uses one.</span></p>

      <article class="entry side" id="e-fix">
        <div class="etext">
          <p class="where">Reader and Research · iPhone, iPad and Mac</p>
          <h3><span class="n">20.</span>Keep your notes in step with corrections</h3>
          <div class="ebody">
            <p>The Office of the Historian sometimes corrects volumes after it publishes them. When a corrected volume arrives, the app compares it with the copy it indexed before. If a document you've marked has changed, a banner says whether the text changed or only the footnotes, source note or heading. Highlights made on the old text turn amber, and Research lists the document under <b class="ui">Changed by an update</b> until you review it.</p>
            <p><b class="ui">Review…</b> opens a sheet where you decide each case. <b class="ui">Confirm</b> keeps a highlight where it is. <b class="ui">Move Here</b> appears only when the app finds your exact words in one new place, and it shows you the passage first. The app never moves a highlight on its own.</p>
            <p>Each quotation you saved from the document as part of a collection is checked again. The same check runs on every quotation in a collection before it exports. It forgives line breaks and curly quotes, but not changed words, so a paraphrase doesn't pass.</p>
            <p class="limit">The check warns but never blocks. When it can't check a quotation, it says why instead of calling it wrong. A document that a correction from the Office of the Historian removed from its volume keeps everything you attached to it.</p>
            <p class="prov"><span class="pk ed">From the editors</span><span>The corrections are the Office's. The app shows what changed and leaves every decision to you.</span></p>
