<section class="pg-sec">
  <h2>Built on the editors' work</h2>
  <p>FRUS is an edited documentary history series produced by the State Department's Office of the Historian. The historians who compile the volumes select documents for publication after extensive research in classified records from across the foreign affairs interagency and then add editorial annotation to help readers place each document in its contemporary and archival context. They give each document a heading, a source note describing its original provenance and current archival location, and add other footnotes as needed to help readers understand the text and locate other directly related records. The digital editions published by the State Department include all of that work as open TEI data.</p>
  <p>FRUS Explorer builds on that foundation to transform editorial annotation into active tools that help researchers reach and share insights about the history of U.S. foreign policy. It turns informative text into direct links to archives, graphs of incoming and outgoing cross-references, cross-volume people networks, and visualized analytics. Because FRUS editors' practices changed over 165 years, not every volume carries every kind of annotation. Every volume's page shows what kinds of annotation it carries, even before you download it, and Browse lays out each kind of annotation across the series' eras.</p>
  <p>The app clearly labels where the information on screen came from, so users can distinguish at a glance what came directly from the published volumes, what came from other Office of the Historian data sources or the National Archives catalog, and what was built specifically for the app.</p>

<section class="pg-sec">
  <h2>Questions</h2>
  <dl class="pg-faq">
    <dt>Is this an official government app?</dt>
    <dd>No. FRUS Explorer is an independent research tool released in the developer's private capacity. It is not an official product of the Office of the Historian or the U.S. Department of State. The FRUS series itself is in the public domain.</dd>
    <dt>When will it be out?</dt>
    <dd>Soon. This page will link to the App Store as soon as the app is there.</dd>
    <dt>What does it cost?</dt>
    <dd>It will be free.</dd>
    <dt>What do I need?</dt>
    <dd>An iPhone or iPad with iOS or iPadOS 26 or later, or a Mac with macOS 26 or later. Document summaries also need Apple Intelligence.</dd>
    <dt>Does it need the internet?</dt>
    <dd>Only to download volumes and a few optional extras. After that, reading and searching work offline. If you add NARA Catalog and/or Zotero API keys, those connections also require internet connectivity.</dd>
    <dt>How much space does it take?</dt>
    <dd>The whole series is about 3.3 GB. The search index the app builds takes about 2.8 times as much again. You can download only the volumes you need, and remove them later.</dd>
    <dt>Does it use AI?</dt>
    <dd>Two optional features use a model on your device. Apple Intelligence writes document summaries, and each one is labeled as generated. Search by meaning relies on a downloaded embedding model to encode your queries. Nothing you type leaves your device for either of these features.</dd>
    <dt>Where do I start?</dt>
    <dd>The quick start for <a href="/quick-start-ios">iPad and iPhone</a> or for <a href="/quick-start-mac">the Mac</a> takes you through a first session, from the first download to a cited document.</dd>
    <dt>Which languages does it support?</dt>
    <dd>English only, just like the FRUS series itself.</dd>
  </dl>
