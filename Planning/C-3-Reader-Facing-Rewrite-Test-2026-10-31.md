# The rewrite test of R1 and R2 alone, on the reader-facing tier's research — results

**Status:** RUN 2026-10-31, on the owner's word ("do the smaller test of r1 and r2 alone", then "8
pairs now", with a stop line at 97% of the weekly Fable meter). The readings were written before any
writer ran (`c3-reader-facing-tier/rewrite/READINGS.md`). Record: `c3-reader-facing-tier/rewrite/`.

**Why.** The reader-facing tier (`C-3-Reader-Facing-Tier-Results-2026-10-31.md`) found that W's
writing changed in every run, but its judges ranked three different research sessions at a time and
weighed the evidence first, so W's reader reading could not isolate the writing. This test holds the
research fixed. It is the pilot's design (`Agentic-Guide-Reader-Facing-Style-Review-2026-10-30.md`
§5) on that tier's eight A and K memos.

**Adopted, 2026-10-31.** On the owner's word ("Include in 1819, along with adopting R1 and R2"), R1
and R2 are in the guide at v1.40 (§12's READER-FACING OUTPUT and §14.16's template).

**The answer.** All six readings hold. Written from the same memo, the overview under R1 and R2 was
preferred by the blind judge in 7 of 8 pairs.

## 1. What was run

- **Eight pairs**, one for each A and K run of the tier. Each run's memo and collection were written
  into an overview twice by the run's own model (Opus 5.5 at `medium` or Sonnet 5.5 at `high`):
  - once under the READER-FACING OUTPUT section and the skeleton as they stand (v1.38);
  - once under R1, the style review's 30-line §4 text at `848f3781` (`d08e51eb…`), with R2's four
    skeleton replacements. No vocabulary bullet: R1 and R2 alone.
- **One blind pairwise judge a pair**, on Fable 5.1 at `max`, with the order seeded: J1–J5 (J3 and
  J5 as the pilot worded them), one citation form, tool words, and a preference with its reason.
- **The script checks** of the tier on every rewrite: `style_measure.py`, and `o2p_check.py` against
  the writer's own trace table.
- **Cost:** 3 points of the weekly all-models meter (55% to 58%) and 4 of the Fable meter (87% to
  91%).

shorter prose and closing list "read more easily".

## 3. What it shows, and what it does not

- **The writing.** With the research held fixed, R1 and R2 produce overviews the judge prefers,
  shorter sentences, a bounded first paragraph, one citation form and fewer untraced numbers. This
  replicates the pilot (4 of 4) on a new question, on the revised R1, and on twice the pairs.
- **The cost to the research is not measured here.** A writer from a finished memo cannot lose a
  research item. The tier's reading 5 stands as measured: W lost six of the 35 items against A's
  four, all on research procedure (S3, P2, P6, R3), at n = 4 an arm.
- **So the decision is the owner's, with both in hand.** By the tier's rule, which the owner
  followed, R1 and R2 are not adopted. This test does not reverse that rule. It says that the
  writing gain is real when the research is the same.

## 4. Limitations

1. The judge's blinding is partial: R1's closing list shows which overview is which.
2. One question, eight memos, one judge a pair.
3. The writers worked from finished memos, as the pilot's did, not under the whole block while
   researching.

## 5. The overview and the collection together

The owner asked whether the overviews, read with the collection files, point the reader to the
documents each run identified. Each overview's linked citations were compared with its collection's
`document` entries.

- **No overview cites a document its collection lacks.** That held in all 28: the tier's twelve and
  the test's sixteen.
- **In the tier, where each session wrote both files**, the four R1 and R2 runs cited every
  collected document: 86 of 86, so the Documents cited list and the collection name the same
  documents. The current wording left documents in the collection that its overview never names: 14
  of 111 in A (9 of them in one run) and 16 of 104 in K (16 in one run).
- **In the test, where the collection was fixed and only the overview rewritten**, R1 and R2 left 18
  of the 215 collected documents uncited, against 14 under the current wording. The pairs differ
  both ways (P1 7 against 5, P6 5 against 0, P7 0 against 5).
- **The path to a document differs.** Under R1 and R2, the text gives a short citation and the
  Documents cited list gives the full citation and link; the collection opens the documents in the
  app. Under the current wording the link sits in the sentence, which two judges preferred because
  the reader can check without leaving the page.
- **Not counted:** documents a run read and set aside. The collection holds only what the argument
  rests on, by design, so those are named only in the memo, which the historian does not receive.
