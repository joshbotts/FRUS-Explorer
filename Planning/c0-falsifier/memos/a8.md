## 1. The headline: this is a well-documented question, and FRUS names it itself

The single most useful thing I found is that **the editors gave this subject its own compilation
in forty-one chapters across thirty-nine volumes**, and their phrasing is a repeating formula.
Read their headings before writing any query of your own:

```sql
SELECT vs.volume_id, jt.value FROM volume_structures vs, json_tree(vs.structure_json) jt
WHERE jt.key='title' AND (lower(jt.value) LIKE '%narcotic%' OR lower(jt.value) LIKE '%opium%'
   OR lower(jt.value) LIKE '%dangerous drug%' OR lower(jt.value) LIKE '%habit-forming drug%'
   OR lower(jt.value) LIKE '%drug control%' OR lower(jt.value) LIKE '%importation of drugs%');
```

The formula moves through four phases, and the phases *are* the periodisation:

| Era | The editors' recurring heading | Volumes |
|---|---|---|
| 1906–16 | "Restriction upon the importation, growth, and use of opium"; "Joint International Commission for the Investigation of the Opium Question in the Far East"; "First/Second/Third International Opium Conference" | `frus1906p1`, `frus1907p1`, `frus1908`–`frus1916`, `frus1915Supp`, `frus1916Supp` |
| 1918–29 | "Opium and morphine traffic in China"; "Participation by delegates from the United States in the deliberations of the League of Nations Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium"; "Conference for Control of the Traffic in Habit-Forming Drugs" | `frus1918`, `frus1923v01`–`frus1929v01` |
| 1931–39 | "Convention … for limiting the manufacture and regulating the distribution of narcotic drugs"; "Conference on the Suppression of Opium Smoking"; **"Problem of controlling the traffic in opium and narcotic drugs in China, including Manchuria and Jehol"** (repeated annually 1934–39) | `frus1931v01`–`frus1939v04` |
| 1944–63 | "Initiative of the United States to secure cooperation among interested governments on measures to limit and control production and to suppress illicit traffic in opium"; "the problem of illicit traffic in narcotics" (Turkey); "Narcotics" | `frus1944v02`, `frus1944v05`, `frus1945v02`, `frus1947v08`, `frus1952-54v08`, `frus1961-63v25` |

**Sized:**

```sql
-- distinct documents inside those compilations, apparatus excluded
-- 656 documents, 39 volumes;  527 documents, 38 volumes with frus:doc-dateTime-min < 1970
```

The 1970s compilations (`frus1969-76ve01`, 85 docs; `frus1969-76ve03`, 60 docs) are outside
your period but worth knowing about as the terminus your story runs into.

### A defect in that inventory you should know about

Two compilations the editors *named* return **zero** documents from the structure index because
their `documentIds` array is empty:

```
frus1936v01 $.sections[1].subsections[21] documentIds = []
  "Participation by the United States in the Conference for the Suppression of the
   Illicit Traffic in Dangerous Drugs, Geneva, June 8–26, 1936"
frus1944v05  "Initiative of the United States … in areas of the Far East to be liberated
   from Japanese control"
```

The documents exist — the free-text scan finds 1 in `frus1936v01` and 5 in `frus1944v05`
(e.g. `frus1944v05/d895`, `frus1944v05/d903`, `frus1944v05/d906`, Steinhardt in Ankara to the
Secretary). So **the compilation inventory is a floor, not a census.** Use it to find the
chapters; use the text scan to find the documents.

## 2. What I would search for — and one term you must not search for

### The false-friend test (mandatory, and it failed for the obvious term)

```sql
-- denominator: pre-1970 documents, apparatus excluded, Ed2 suppressed
-- A. all pre-1970 docs:            276,895 | has "Department of State" 80,698 | has "control" 47,493
-- B. pre-1970 narcotics-vocab docs:  1,081 | has "Department of State"    297 | has "control"    410
```

* **"Department of State": 297 of 1,081 (27.5%) against a baseline of 80,698 of 276,895
  (29.1%).** It runs *below* baseline. The phrase your question supplied is measuring the
  corpus, not the question. Do not use it as a discriminator anywhere in this project.
* **"control": 410 of 1,081 (37.9%) against 47,493 of 276,895 (17.2%)** — about 2.2× baseline,
  which is real but far too weak to retrieve on. "Narcotics control" as a phrase is also
  substantially a *post-1960* coinage; see §5.

Four things to take from that table:

1. **`morphia` (157 occurrences, 16 volumes) is the 19th- and early-20th-century form** and no
   modern search will find it. Likewise `laudanum`. If you scan only modern vocabulary you will
   manufacture a pre-1906 absence that is not there — see §4.
2. **`marihuana` (47) outnumbers `marijuana` (33)**, and both are trivial. Cannabis is not what
   this corpus is about before 1970; opiates and coca are.
3. **The acronyms behave oppositely to the usual rule.** `CND` (83) is a real presence;
   `FBN` (3 occurrences, 1 volume) and "Federal Bureau of Narcotics" (2 occurrences) are almost
   absent — which is itself the institutional finding in §6.
4. **"Permanent Central Board" (44, 3 vols) outnumbers "Permanent Central Opium Board"
   (31, 4 vols).** Query both or you lose more than half the body.

### A method artefact I caught, because you should not trust the table without it

My first TEI pass returned **0** for `narcotic drug(s)`, `opium traffic` and `opium poppy` —
which would have been a striking and completely false finding. Python's alternation is
leftmost-first, not longest-match, so the bare `opium` and `narcotics?` branches earlier in my
combined pattern consumed the phrases before the phrase branches could match. The numbers above
come from a re-run in which the phrases are scanned without the single words competing
(`/tmp/tei_scan2.py`). **A zero from a phrase scan is a claim about your regex until you have
proved otherwise.** Both passes carried the same controls, which is how the artefact was
visible at all: the positive control was unaffected while three phrases dropped to nothing.

### The recommended query

```
narcotic OR opium OR opiate OR morphine OR morphia OR cocaine OR heroin OR hashish
  OR laudanum OR marihuana OR marijuana OR "coca leaf" OR "dangerous drugs"
  OR "habit-forming drugs" OR "opium poppy" OR "poppy cultivation"
```

In this index (porter-stemmed, so `narcotic` also retrieves `narcotics`), apparatus excluded,
Ed2 suppressed, that yields **1,626 documents in 271 volumes** corpus-wide, of which
**1,081 documents fall before 1970** (periodised on `document_dates.date_iso`, which is
`frus:doc-dateTime-min`, **not** the volume's series year).

**1,081 of 276,895 pre-1970 documents.** This is a small, sharply concentrated subject, not a
diffuse one.

## 4. The pre-1906 material the compilation index cannot see

`frus1894app2` (41 documents), `frus1887` (20) and `frus1888p1` (12) rank in the top twenty and
have **no narcotics compilation heading at all**. Their document headers are bare — "No. 190.
Mr. Bayard to Mr. Chang Yen Hoon" — so the subject is discoverable only in body text. Retrieved
snippets confirm the content is the enforcement of the opium article of the 1880 Sino-American
treaty and the implementing US statute:

* `frus1887/d136` — "Is the storage of [opium] by an…" (the consul-general's question)
* `frus1887/d190` — "Every package of [opium] or package containing [opium], either in whole or
  in part, brought, taken or transported, trafficked, or dealt in contrary to…"
* `frus1887/d191` — "The ways and measures used by China to deal or treat with imported
  [opium]…" (Chang Yen Hoon, the Chinese Minister, to Bayard)

This is a *different* departmental role from the one the 20th-century compilations show — not
multilateral treaty-making but the policing of American citizens' conduct under an existing
bilateral treaty, conducted through the legation in Peking (Denby) and the Chinese legation in
Washington. If your question is "what did State *do*", these ~120 documents in the 1880s–90s
volumes are the earliest answer and they are invisible to every chapter-heading approach.

## 5. Archival scope — two channels, never summed

### Channel A — CAME-FROM (`document_sources`, one row per document)

Of the 1,081 pre-1970 narcotics documents, **855 carry a source note**:

| citation form | docs |
|---|---:|
| decimal | 724 |
| structured | 63 |
| published | 28 |
| lot_file | 21 |
| unrecognized | 11 |
| named_series | 8 |

*(`citation_era` is a citation **form**, not a date. I have not plotted it as a timeline.)*

**Resolved through `decimal-class-labels.json` (1910–49 schedule), the 560 pre-1970 narcotics
documents that carry a parsed class distribute as:**

```
class 5 Congresses and Conferences (not country-arranged)   204
China                                                       134
United States                                                64
class 3 Protection of Interests                              28
Asia 17 | Iran 12 | Japan 11 | France 6 | Turkey 6 | Mexico 5 | Egypt 5 | USSR 3
```

So the pre-1970 State Department narcotics record is, in its own filing, **overwhelmingly a
conference-and-convention file plus a China file.** That is the thesis the archive itself
proposes.

**The three class families, corpus-wide, came-from channel** (from `collection-usage-index.json`,
cross-validated against this database — see §7):

| family | docs | distinct keys | volumes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `*.114*` — "Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs" | 451 | 27 | 44 |
| `511.4*` — the opium conference file | 165 | 24 | 26 |
| `500.C119*` — League opium committee | 63 | 3 | 11 |

**Two warnings about using these as retrieval keys:**

1. **`.114` is not a narcotics class.** The 1910–49 schedule defines suffix `.114` as *"Traffic
   in liquors and habit-forming drugs"* — one class for two prohibitions. Of its 451 documents,
   **207 carry liquor vocabulary and only 89 carry narcotics vocabulary** (186 carry neither).
   `.114` is substantially the *Prohibition* file. Pulling it as a narcotics key would give you
   a majority-irrelevant set. The narcotics-specific spellings within it are worth noting,
   though: NARA/FRUS actually writes `891.114Narcotics` (Iran), `893.114-N` and `893.114N`
   (China), `800.114N16`.
2. **`511.4*` is a clean key and it beats the words.** 130 of its 165 documents also carry
   narcotics vocabulary — and **35 do not**, i.e. the class recovers 35 documents the text scan
   misses entirely. These are the procedural skin of conference diplomacy that never names the
   drug: `frus1914/d1641` (Netherlands Minister to the Secretary), `frus1914/d1648` and
   `/d1650` (Delegate Van Dyke to the Secretary), `frus1923v01/d40` (Secretary to the Consul at
   Geneva), `frus1924v01/d51` (Secretary General of the League), `frus1925v02/d555`,
   `frus1928v01/d308` (Secretary to President Coolidge), `frus1931v01/d513`. **Search the file
   number as well as the word.**

### Channel B — POINTED-AT (`external_citations`, many rows per document)

**73 citation rows across 47 documents** — thin, and structurally so. By repository: Department
of State 67, Kennedy Library 3, Eisenhower 1, Johnson 1, Nixon 1. Only two lot files appear
(`54D423` ×6, `62D181` ×1). The reason is chronological: footnote lot-file and presidential-
library citation is a *post-1945* filing practice, and your period's centre of gravity is
1906–1945. **This channel is close to useless for the pre-war half of your question, and that
is a property of the citation practice, not of the corpus.** Report the two channels separately;
never add 855 and 73.

One trap in the little that is there: **lot `54D423` is a divided lot.** `central-files-index.json`
names one series ("Japanese Petitions", NAID 2127217, entry A1 1255), but
`lot-claimants-index.json` shows **four** RG 59 claimants on the same control number — Subject
Files (2127213, A1 1252), Cable Files (2127215, A1 1253), Japanese Comments on the Peace Treaty
(2127216, A1 1254), Japanese Petitions. A single-card answer here would be wrong.

### The concrete pre-1910 roadmap: Numerical File case 774

This is the most actionable archival result, and it comes from reading the source notes rather
than the class field. The 1906–10 volumes' notes read **"File No. 774/393."** — the *Numerical
File* case-number form, which this database's parser labels `citation_era='decimal'` while
leaving `decimal_class` **empty**. Extracting the case numbers:

```sql
-- case number -> narcotics documents, frus1906p1..frus1914
-- 774: 139 docs across 4 volumes    511: 40    1518: 4    1571: 3    893: 2 ...
```

**Case 774 is the opium case file: 139 documents.** Resolved through
`central-files-index.json → numericalFile` (series NAID **654171**, microfilm publication
**M862**), case 774 sits on five rolls:

| roll title | NAID |
|---|---|
| Numerical File: 774–774/180 | 19179039 |
| Numerical File: 774/181–774/350 | 19179783 |
| Numerical File: 774/351–774/524 | 19180769 |
| Numerical File: 774/525–774/675 | 19181571 |
| Numerical File: 774/676–785/5 | 19182438 |

**A corpus-scoping negative is not a research negative:** FRUS printed 139 documents from case
774; the case itself is these five rolls, and FRUS's own citations tell you which item within
them. This is the single highest-value archival lead in the memo.

### What the offline stack cannot do for you

* **`series-facts-index.json` holds 695 series; exactly 1 has an end year before 1940, and 2
  before 1945.** The bundled series apparatus does not reach your period. The Numerical File
  series (654171) is not in it at all.
* **`digitized-ranges-index.json` covers 18 decimal classes, all in the 131/133 (visa) and 763
  (WWI Europe) families. None of `511.4*`, `*.114*` or `500.C119*` is digitised.** Expect a
  reading-room trip, not a download.

## 6. The institutional point the corpus makes by omission

`persons` contains **exactly one** entry for Anslinger — `frus1961-63v25/p_AHJ2`, "Anslinger,
Harvey J., **Commissioner of Narcotics, Department of the Treasury**" (note the given name is
wrong in the FRUS list; he was Harry J.). He is named in 18 documents across 11 volumes.
"Federal Bureau of Narcotics" occurs **twice in the whole corpus**; `FBN` three times in one
volume.

The man who *was* American narcotics policy for thirty-two years is nearly invisible here
because his bureau was in **Treasury**, and FRUS prints the *State* Department's file. That is
not a defect in the corpus — it is the answer to your question in negative. What FRUS documents
is the diplomatic half of a divided competence: delegations and credentials, convention
drafting and ratification, League and UN representation, and démarches to producer and transit
states (China, Iran, Turkey, Mexico, Japan-in-China). Enforcement, licensing and domestic
control were somebody else's file, and you will not find them here.

Corroborating that reading: `frus1961-63v25`'s narcotics compilation names Anslinger 6 times
and **A. Gilmore Flues, Assistant Secretary of the *Treasury*,** in 8 documents.

**Do not use `person_mentions` for the pre-1945 half of this.** The top-ranked names over the
pre-1970 narcotics set are Nixon, Rogers, Kissinger, Dulles, U. Alexis Johnson, Rusk — plus a
spurious cluster of 1945 UN Conference delegates (Stettinius, Pasvolsky, Vandenberg,
Gildersleeve, Bloom, Connally, Eaton, Stassen) who appear only because `frus1945v01` is densely
`<persName>`-tagged and narcotics turns up incidentally in an ECOSOC functions list. TEI person
tagging is uneven across volumes and covers a small share of mentions; these are lower bounds
skewed hard toward the volumes that happen to be tagged.

## 7. Things I got wrong, so you can check me

1. **I misread `collection-usage-index.json`'s wire format.** The rows are `{k, n, v}`; I first
   read `n` as volume indices and `v` as counts, which produced a ranking in which the *Carter
   Library* supplied 514 documents to *frus1865p1*. The correct orientation is **`v` = volume
   index, `n` = count**. I validated it two ways before using any number: every `v` is < 552 and
   every `n` ≥ 1; and class `893.114`'s per-volume counts from the JSON
   (`frus1936v04` 10, `frus1918` 10, `frus1935v03` 7, `frus1934v03` 7, `frus1937v04` 5,
   `frus1939v04` 3, `frus1917` 2, `frus1944v06` 1, `frus1930v02` 1) match this database's
   `document_sources` exactly. Every JSON-derived figure in §5 is post-correction.
2. **The volume-scoped archival ranking is the wrong instrument for this question and I dropped
   it.** `collection-usage-index.json` is at *volume* grain, and these are mostly general annual
   volumes where narcotics is one chapter of forty. Ranking archival collections over the 39
   compilation volumes returns 34,422 documents dominated by presidential-library files —
   measuring the volumes, not the subject. The document-grain came-from figures in §5 come from
   this database, where I can restrict to the narcotics documents themselves.
3. **The phrase-scan zeros in §2** were a regex artefact, corrected and re-run.
4. **The compilation inventory under-reports** (§1), by two chapters that I found only because
   the free-text channel disagreed with it.

## 8. What I did not do

* I have **not** separated body text from editorial footnotes. `body_text` in this index
  contains both, and `cross_references.reference_type` defaults body references to `'footnote'`,
  so it cannot support the split either. Every frequency above blends the editors' language with
  the historical actors'. Given that FRUS annotation is heavy and modern, **treat any
  20th-century "narcotics control" hit as possibly the editor speaking.** Doing this properly
  means a TEI pass that excludes `<note>` — worth an hour if you intend to publish a density.
* I have **not** verified any NARA creator heading. The house rule that creator attribution is a
  decoy applies; I have reported series and entry numbers, which are NARA's own assertions,
  rather than creator claims.
* Subject tags (`document_subject_refs`) are string-matched candidates and I did not use them.
  `subject_tag_ids` is always NULL.

## 9. Suggested order of work

1. **`frus1961-63v25` "Narcotics" (18 body docs, 1961-01-03 → 1963-06-28)** — small, explicit,
   and the only compilation in your period that carries the modern name. Read it first to
   calibrate the vocabulary and to see the State/Treasury division stated plainly.
2. **The 1906–10 core** (`frus1906p1` 17, `frus1907p1` 39, `frus1908` 51, `frus1909` 30,
   `frus1910` 54) — the Shanghai Commission and the Hague Convention, i.e. the moment the
   Department invented the multilateral instrument. Then Numerical File case 774 at NARA.
3. **The China spine** — the annual "Problem of controlling the traffic in opium and narcotic
   drugs in China, including Manchuria and Jehol" chapters, 1934–39, plus decimal `893.114` /
   `893.114-N`. This is where the subject stops being treaty diplomacy and becomes an
   accusation against Japanese occupation policy.
4. **The 1880s bilateral material** in `frus1887`, `frus1888p1`, `frus1894app2` — no compilation,
   no headers, body text only, and no source notes worth the name (`frus1887` and `frus1894app2`
   have essentially none; `frus1888p1` has a single `unrecognized` row). This is the hardest
   material to find and the least likely to have been worked over.
5. **The 1950s–60s trough** — 76 and 69 documents. Ask why the selection thinned; the answer is
   probably that the file moved to the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs and to Treasury, and FRUS
   follows State.
