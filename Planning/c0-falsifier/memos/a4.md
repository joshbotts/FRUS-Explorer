# Scoping memo: the Department of State and international narcotics control before 1970

**Corpus:** local FRUS Explorer index — 316,839 documents, 552 volumes, plus the TEI XML the index was built from and the app's bundled reference JSON.
**Date of pass:** 2026-08-31. All figures below were measured, not estimated; the queries are given so you can re-run them.
**Status:** scoping pass, ~35 queries. Not a finished survey.

---

## 1. Headline

FRUS carries this topic well, and it carries it in a shape you should know about before you start reading.

Between 1906 and 1947 the Office of the Historian treated international narcotics control as a **standing named compilation** — a titled chapter that recurs, volume after volume, with explicit "For previous correspondence, see Foreign Relations, 19xx" continuity notes. I count **40 such compilation headings across 35 volumes, holding 498 documents**, running in an almost unbroken annual chain from *For. Rel. 1906* to *For. Rel. 1947*.

Then it stops. **Not one document dated 1948–1960 sits in a narcotics-titled compilation** — against 64,632 corpus documents dated in that window. The thread resumes once, thinly, as a 20-document chapter simply titled "Narcotics" in *FRUS 1961–1963* vol. XXV, and then explodes after 1969 in the Nixon-era volumes, which are outside your period.

So the corpus gives you a rich, editorially curated run for **1906–1947**, a real but shallow **1961–63** window, and a **1948–60 hole that is a hole in FRUS's selection, not in the archive** — the Department was demonstrably busy in exactly those years (the Single Convention was drafted from 1948; the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs met annually), and the 1961–63 documents cite the Central Files that would hold it.

The role FRUS documents State playing, across the whole pre-1970 span, is consistently the same one: **State was the convening, negotiating and instructing department, not the enforcing one.** The enforcement agency — Treasury's Bureau of Narcotics under Anslinger — appears in these files as a client State clears instructions for, not as the author of policy abroad. One document states this almost too neatly; see §5.

## 2. What I actually measured

Four independent measures, deliberately chosen so they can disagree with each other.

### (a) Full-text keyword hits (FTS5, porter-stemmed)

```sql
SELECT count(*) FROM frus_documents WHERE frus_documents MATCH
 'narcotic OR narcotics OR opium OR cocaine OR morphine OR heroin
  OR marihuana OR marijuana OR cannabis OR hashish OR "dangerous drugs"';
```
1,676 documents total. Joined to `document_dates`: **1,087 dated pre-1970**, 538 dated 1970+, 51 undated. They touch 211 distinct volumes, so the tail is very long and very thin.

By decade (`substr(date_iso,1,3)||'0s'`):

| 1860s | 1870s | 1880s | 1890s | 1900s | 1910s | 1920s | 1930s | 1940s | 1950s | 1960s | 1970s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | 33 | 64 | 54 | **161** | 142 | 136 | **187** | 132 | 78 | 74 | 460 |

### (b) The Office of the Historian's own subject taxonomy

The bundled `document-subject-index.json` carries a curated subject group **"Narcotics"** under the category *Global Issues*, with eight terms and their corpus document-frequencies: Opium (1,016), Narcotics (631), Heroin (175), Narcotics control (168), Cocaine (100), Morphine (99), Narcotics traffic (69), Drug industry (8). These are subject ids 364, 107, 481, 395, 114, 321, 264, 415 in `document_subject_refs`.

Union of the eight, joined to dates: **1,016 documents dated pre-1970.** The decade curve tracks measure (a) to within a few percent and the per-volume ranking is near-identical. **Two independently built indexes agreeing this closely is the main reason I trust the shape of the finding.**

### (c) Compilation headings (the editors' own chapter titles)

Parsed every volume's `volume_structures.structure_json` table of contents for titles matching `narcotic|opium|habit-forming|dangerous drugs`. 40 sections, 35 volumes, **498 documents, every one of them dated before 1970.** Full table in §7.

### (d) Archival provenance — the Department's own file classes

`document_sources.raw_text` / `decimal_class`. This is the sharpest instrument, because the State Department filed narcotics under identifiable numbers and FRUS prints the file number in the source note:

| File class | What it is | Docs |
|---|---|---|
| `774/…` | 1906–10 Numerical File, opium case | **150** |
| `511.4A*`, `511.4R1` | Class 5 (Congresses and Conferences), opium/narcotics conventions | ~158 |
| `500.C1197` | League of Nations, Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium | 33 |
| `NNN.114 Narcotics/…` | country file, "Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs" (893 = China, 890 = Far East general, 891 = Persia/Iran) | 58 |
| `341.9` | the 1950–63 Central Files narcotics class | 13 |
| `SOC 11-5` | the 1963–73 subject-numeric narcotics class | 52 |

The `.114` gloss is not my invention — `decimal-class-labels.json` (parsed from NARA's *Classification of Correspondence, August 1910 – December 1949*) gives subject suffix `114` = **"Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs"** under class 8, Internal Affairs of States, and class 5 = "Congresses and Conferences."

## 3. Four traps in this data, all of which I hit

**(i) `.114` is a liquor class as much as a drug class.** `811.114` (the United States file) holds **268 documents, of which only 7 mention any narcotics term and 216 mention liquor/Prohibition/smuggling.** It is the Prohibition-era rum-running file. If you filter on `decimal_class LIKE '%.114%'` you will import the entire Coast Guard/rum-row correspondence. By contrast `893.114` (China) is 44/46 narcotics. The country number is doing all the work.

**(ii) `511.4` is a family, not a topic.** `511.4C1` (7 docs) is the St. Germain-en-Laye convention revising the Berlin and Brussels Acts — **liquor traffic in Africa, zero narcotics mentions.** All the `511.4A*` sub-files and `511.4R1` are genuinely narcotics (48/51, 28/31, 19/28, 9/9, 8/8, 7/8). Also watch transcription variants: `511.4Al` and `511.4a6` appear alongside `511.4A1`/`511.4A6`, and the pre-WWII volumes print the class with spaces (`511.4 A 2/31b`) while the parser normalises inconsistently.

**(iii) "drugs" without a qualifier is usually pharmaceuticals.** *FRUS 1915 Supplement* and *1916 Supplement* both carry a section literally titled **"Importation of drugs"** — 17 documents about salvarsan, neosalvarsan and novocaine caught in the British blockade (`File No. 300.115/…`). *FRUS 1920* vol. II has a 44-document section on "chemical drugs," which is German dyestuffs. All false positives.

**(iv) A large slice of the 19th-century tagging is not narcotics-control material.** The single biggest pre-1900 concentration is ***FRUS 1894, Appendix II*, 40 documents** — Stevens's Honolulu dispatches to Blaine and Foster, i.e. the **Hawaiian opium licence affair** as ammunition in the overthrow narrative. Domestic Hawaiian politics, not international control. Read it if you want the origins of the "opium as pretext" repertoire; skip it if you want treaty diplomacy.

## 4. The shape of the pre-1970 record, period by period

**1880s–1905 — treaty enforcement against Americans.** ~150 documents, mostly Denby–Bayard from the Peking legation, on the **1880 treaty's Article 2** and the **Act of 23 February 1887** forbidding US citizens from the China opium trade. `frus1887` d148 is the type case: Bayard approving Denby's action against Russell & Co. as "contrary to the intent and spirit of the treaty of 1880." State's role here is *policing its own nationals under a bilateral treaty* — the pre-history, and worth reading as such.

**1906–1914 — State as convener.** The densest and most self-contained run in the whole pre-1970 record: **~190 documents in `frus1906p1`, `frus1907p1`, `frus1908`, `frus1909`, `frus1910`, `frus1911`, `frus1912`–`1915`.** Opium is the **#1-ranked subject of `frus1908`** and #2 of `frus1907p1` and `frus1909` in the bundled `volume-subject-profiles-index.json`. This is State organising the **Shanghai Commission (1909)** and the **Hague conferences (1911–14)** by circular telegram out of Washington to London, Paris, Berlin, The Hague, Tokyo, Peking, St. Petersburg, Rome and Lisbon. `frus1908` d72 (`File No. 774/197A`, Root to Reid, 7 May 1908, repeated to six posts) is the founding instrument: the United States "finds itself invited to suggest the date and place of meeting, and it therefore names Shanghai and January 1, 1909," with three commissioners and a $20,000 appropriation to be asked of Congress. The chain begins earlier and more modestly — `frus1906p1` d332, Bacon to Rockhill, 11 July 1906, is a routine courtesy for a missionary that ends by asking for "a report from you on the present aspects of the opium trade in China."

**1918 — a discrete episode.** 10 documents on China's purchase and destruction of the reserve opium stock.

**1923–1939 — State inside the League it had not joined.** This is the most analytically interesting stretch. The United States was not a League member, yet FRUS documents a continuous American presence in the League's drug machinery: 14 documents on sending delegates to the **Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium** (1923, `511.4 A 1/…`); 26 on the **1924–25 Geneva conference** (`511.4 A 2/…`), including Stephen Porter's telegrams as chairman of the American delegation and a printed "Suggestions of the United States Presented to the Second Opium Conference"; 30 on the **1931 Limitation Convention** plus 8 on the **Bangkok Opium Smoking conference**; and a six-year serial compilation, 1934–1939, on "**the traffic in opium and narcotic drugs in China, including Manchuria and Jehol**" (57 documents), which is narcotics policy fused with the indictment of Japanese occupation. The 1923 file also contains State–Treasury correspondence (Hughes to Mellon, `511.4 A 1/1707a`) about who supplies the delegate — the turf question in its earliest documented form.

**1940 — the orphaned League bodies, and it is filed where you will not look.** `frus1940v02` carries 20 subject-tagged documents on keeping the **Permanent Central Opium Board and the Drug Supervisory Body** alive after Geneva collapsed. Breckinridge Long to Roosevelt, 10 September 1940 (`511.4A2A/1008`): these are "derivatives of the League of Nations, but in effect agencies for the enforcement of opium control," and they "desire to retire to the United States." **The compilation is titled "Interest of the United States in proposals for the reorganization of the non-political activities of the League of Nations."** A search on narcotics chapter titles misses it entirely.

**1943–1947 — the American postwar initiative.** `frus1944v02` (20 docs) + `frus1945v02` (3): "Initiative of the United States to secure cooperation among interested governments on measures to limit and control production and to suppress illicit traffic in opium," filed at `890.114 Narcotics/…`. Correspondents are the Netherlands and British embassies, Gauss in Chungking, the Chinese ambassador, and — significantly — the Secretary of War. This is the campaign to abolish colonial opium monopolies in reoccupied Southeast Asia. Then `frus1947v08`, 3 documents on the **Mexican border traffic**.

**1948–1960 — the gap.** Zero compilation documents; **82 subject-tagged documents**, against 64,632 corpus documents in the window (0.13%, versus 0.44% for 1906–47). What survives is *incidental and embedded*, and it is genuinely worth having:
- **The Japanese Peace Treaty drafts.** `frus1950v06` and `frus1951v06p1` carry the narcotics articles: Japan to "seek adherence to existing multilateral treaties … designed … to prevent the misuse of narcotics," and specifically to accede to "the Protocol amending the 1931 Narcotics Convention signed at New York on December 11th, 1946." Filed `694.001` — the peace-treaty class, not a narcotics class.
- **Turkey.** `frus1952-54v08` has a Turkey compilation whose title ends "…AND THE PROBLEM OF ILLICIT TRAFFIC IN NARCOTICS." Note the trap: that heading contributes 32 documents to my compilation count but only about one of them is narcotics — treat 498 as ~467 if you want a clean figure.
- Scattered singletons: an opium-smuggling allegation in the Indonesia Good Offices telegrams (`frus1948v06`, `501.BC Indonesia/…`), Thailand (`frus1958-60v15`), the Geneva Sino-American ambassadorial talks (`frus1955-57v03mSupp`, `611.93`).

**1961–1963 — one compilation, and it is the best single entry point for the modern question.** `frus1961-63v25`, chapter "Narcotics," 20 documents, under the volume's *Organization and Administration of Foreign Policy* rubric. Contents: Rusk's instructions to the US representative at the **Single Convention** conference; three **Report of the U.S. Delegation to the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs** (16th, 17th, 18th sessions); embassy reporting from **Ankara** and **Athens** on opium production; a memorandum from the head of the UN Narcotics Division; and a letter from the **president of Eli Lilly** to the Commissioner of Narcotics — the pharmaceutical-industry interest, in the file. File numbers `341.9/…` through 1962, then `SOC 11-5` from 1963, which is where the class changes.

**1964–1968 — essentially nothing.** 63 subject-tagged documents across the whole corpus for those years, nearly all incidental (e.g. `frus1964-68v28`, Laos).

## 5. The one document I would put first

`frus1961-63v25` d326 — *Instructions From Secretary of State Rusk to the U.S. Representative to the UN Conference for the Adoption of a Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs (**Anslinger**)*, 19 January 1961, `RG 59, Central Files 1960–63, 341.9/1–1961`. The FRUS editorial note attached to it says the quiet part: "**Anslinger was Commissioner of Narcotics in the Department of the Treasury. The instructions were drafted on January 18 by Chester G. Dunham (IO/OIC) and cleared and approved by Assistant Secretary of State for International Organization Affairs Francis O. Wilcox.**"

That is your thesis in one source note: the most famous drug-control official in American history goes to the founding conference of the modern regime **carrying instructions written and cleared inside State's Bureau of International Organization Affairs.** Whatever State's role was, "subordinate to Treasury" is not it. Note also that `persons` in this corpus renders him "Anslinger, Harvey J." — FRUS's own list of persons has the name wrong, which will matter if you search by name.

## 6. What I would search for, in order

1. **Start from the compilation table in §7, not from keywords.** 498 documents, curated by the editors, is a reading list. Work down it chronologically.
2. **Then search provenance, because it finds what the chapter titles hide.** In this index: `document_sources.raw_text LIKE '%Narcotics/%'`, `LIKE '%511.4A%'`, `LIKE '%500.C1197%'`, `LIKE '%File No. 774%'`, `LIKE '%341.9%'`, `LIKE '%SOC 11-5%'`. In the app, Source Explorer resolves these against NARA. This is what surfaced the 1940 PCOB material and the 1933 St. Germain confusion.
3. **Then the subject union**, ids 107/114/264/321/364/395/415/481, which is the widest defensible net.
4. **Institution phrases, which cut cleanly across all of the above:** `"Opium Advisory Committee"` (46), `"Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium"` (28), `"Commission on Narcotic Drugs"` (51), `"Permanent Central Opium Board"` (15), `"Drug Supervisory Body"` (7), `"Single Convention"` (67), `"Hague Opium Convention"` (22), `"Bureau of Narcotics"` (39), `"opium monopoly"` (31), `Anslinger` (18).
5. **Do not** search bare `drug`, `drugs`, or `smuggling` (1,572 hits, overwhelmingly liquor and general contraband).
6. **For the 1948–60 gap, search the containers, not the topic:** the Japanese Peace Treaty compilations (`694.001`), the Turkey compilations, the ECOSOC/UN General Assembly compilations, and Thailand/Burma/Iran country files. That is where State's narcotics work of those years surfaces in FRUS at all.

## 8. Cross-check that matters most: compilation vs. subject tag

| | count |
|---|---|
| pre-1970 documents in a narcotics-titled compilation | 498 |
| pre-1970 documents carrying an OH narcotics subject tag | 1,016 |
| in both | 415 |
| in a compilation but **not** subject-tagged | 83 |
| subject-tagged but **outside** any narcotics compilation | **601** |

**Read that last row carefully.** Roughly 60% of the pre-1970 narcotics material this corpus knows about is scattered outside the purpose-built chapters — in League-reorganisation compilations, peace-treaty drafts, China and Turkey country files, Hawaii. If you read only the 498, you will get FRUS's editorial *story* about narcotics diplomacy and miss the places where narcotics was an instrument of some other policy. If you read only the 1,016 tagged documents, you will get 40 Hawaiian revolution dispatches and a lot of one-line mentions. **Use both, and expect them to be about half disjoint.**

## 9. Limits of this pass — things I did not do, and things that could be wrong

- **Dates are document dates from `document_dates`, not publication dates.** Two documents in the 1961–63 chapter have no header or date row at all (d336, d342 — probably editorial notes or attachments); they are counted in the compilation total and not in the decade table. 51 keyword hits are undated.
- **The subject taxonomy is the Office of the Historian's, applied unevenly.** Its density is much higher in modern volumes. A pre-1970 volume being lightly tagged is weak evidence of absence.
- **The `persons` table is built from FRUS's own front-matter Lists of Persons**, which only modern-format volumes carry. That is why every narcotics official I could find by role (Anslinger, Giordano, Lande, Chapman, and the whole 1969–80 cast — Gross, Vance, Handley, Falco, Ingersoll) is in a 1961-or-later volume. **You cannot use `persons` to find Hamilton Wright, Bishop Brent, Stuart Fuller or Herbert May in the pre-1950 volumes; they are not in it.** Search the full text for those names instead — I did not, and it is the first thing I would do next.
- **I did not open the TEI XML.** Everything above is from the SQLite index and the bundled JSON. If a compilation heading is malformed in the XML, my §7 table inherits the error.
- **I did not measure the post-1970 side properly**, since it is out of scope — but note that the app's semantic map (`semantic-map-index.json`) has exactly one narcotics cluster, #172, terms `opium / heroin / poppy`, **428 documents of which 426 fall in its later era bucket.** Independent confirmation that the dense, semantically coherent drug material in this corpus is post-1969, and that everything before 1970 is diffuse.
- **`documentCount` in `manifest.json` is 0 for at least some volumes** and should not be trusted as a denominator; I used `document_cache` counts throughout.
- The `.114`/`511.4` traps in §3 were each found by *reading output*, not by reasoning. Assume there are more of them and spot-check any class before you build an argument on its count.

## 10. How to reproduce every number here

```bash
DB='file:/Users/jbotts/frus-analysis/frus-copy.db?mode=ro'
sqlite3 "$DB" "select count(*), count(distinct volume_id) from document_cache;"   # 316839 / 552

# (a) keyword measure
sqlite3 "$DB" "with h as (select volume_id,document_id from frus_documents where frus_documents match
 'narcotic OR narcotics OR opium OR cocaine OR morphine OR heroin OR marihuana OR marijuana
  OR cannabis OR hashish OR \"dangerous drugs\"')
 select sum(d.date_iso<'1970-01-01'), count(*) from h left join document_dates d using(volume_id,document_id);"

# (b) subject measure
sqlite3 "$DB" "with u as (select distinct volume_id,document_id from document_subject_refs
 where subject in (107,114,264,321,364,395,415,481))
 select count(*) from u join document_dates d using(volume_id,document_id) where d.date_iso<'1970-01-01';"

# (d) provenance measure
sqlite3 "$DB" "select count(*) from document_sources where raw_text like '%File No. 774%';"      # 150
sqlite3 "$DB" "select decimal_class,count(*) from document_sources where decimal_class like '511.4%'
 group by 1 order by 2 desc;"

# the liquor trap
sqlite3 "$DB" "with n as (select volume_id,document_id from frus_documents where frus_documents match
 'narcotic OR narcotics OR opium OR heroin OR morphine OR cocaine')
 select count(*), sum(n.document_id is not null) from document_sources s
 left join n using(volume_id,document_id) where s.decimal_class='811.114';"                      # 268 / 7

# the 1948-60 gap
sqlite3 "$DB" "select count(*) from document_dates where date_iso between '1948-01-01' and '1960-12-31';"  # 64632
```
(c) requires walking `volume_structures.structure_json`; the subject ids in (b) come from the `vocab` array of `/Applications/FRUS Explorer.app/Contents/Resources/document-subject-index.json`, and the `.114` gloss from `decimal-class-labels.json`.
