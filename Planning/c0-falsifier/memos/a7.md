# Scoping memo — fishing rights and territorial waters in FRUS

**Question:** How did the United States handle disputes over fishing rights and territorial waters with other governments?

**Bottom line.** This is one of the best-served subjects in the series. It is *continuously* documented from the 1860s to the 1980s, and it is unusual in having its own repeating chapter headings, its own decimal-file classes, its own subject-numeric keys, and — for the modern period — its own dedicated State Department office lots that FRUS cites and names. It also has a nasty stemming trap that will silently ruin a naive keyword search, described in §3.

---

## 2. What the editors call it — read this before writing any query

I walked all 552 `volume_structures` trees for chapter/section headings. **112 headings in 77 volumes** are on-topic. The editors use a small number of repeating formulas, and they are not modern phrasing. The ones worth copying verbatim into searches:

| period | the editors' formula |
|---|---|
| 1872–1912 | "Fisheries in waters adjacent to the British North American possessions"; **"Newfoundland fishery question"** (recurs 1905, 1906, 1907, 1909); "North Atlantic coast fisheries arbitration" (1910, 1912); "Modus vivendi … in regard to Inshore Fisheries on the Treaty coast of Newfoundland" |
| 1880–1886 | "Alleged Outrage upon American Fishermen at Fortune Bay, Newfoundland"; "American Fishing Vessels Molested in Canadian Waters" |
| 1888–1897 | **"Papers relating to the seizure of British sealing-vessels in Behring Sea"**; "Protection of fur seals in Bering Sea"; "Protection of the fur seal" (recurs under Great Britain *and* Russia) |
| 1900–1902 | "Arbitration of claims of American sealing and whaling vessels v. Russia" — and an entire dedicated appendix volume, `frus1902app1`, *Whaling and Sealing Claims Against Russia* |
| 1912 | "Extension by Russia of the three-mile limit of territorial waters to twelve miles for customs purposes and control of fisheries" |

| 1918–1944 | **"Fisheries and related questions"** (Canada, recurring); halibut fishery convention; "Fraser River sockeye salmon fisheries"; Missisquoi Bay; Passamaquoddy Bay; the 1911 four-power fur-seals convention and Japan's 1940 abrogation |
| 1935–1939 | "Ecuadoran fishing regulations purporting to extend the territorial waters"; "Representations to Japan in regard to regulation of fisheries off the coast of Alaska" |
| 1937–1946 | a separate whaling thread: International Conference for the Regulation of Whaling (1937, 1938, 1939, 1943, 1944, 1945, 1946) |
| **1945** | **"Formulation of United States policy on the resources of the Continental Shelf and on coastal fisheries"** — the Truman Proclamations compilation |

That last row is the single most useful fact in this memo: the editors treated law of the sea as a **standing compilation with an explicit hand-off chain**, so following the cross-references between those seven volumes gives you a continuous policy narrative without a keyword search at all.

## 3. The trap you must avoid: `fish` is Hamilton Fish

The index tokenizer is porter, so `fishing`, `fished`, `fishes` and `fish` all fold to the stem **`fish`** — and so does the surname of **Hamilton Fish, Secretary of State 1869–1877**.

```sql
-- docs matching stem 'fish', by volume
frus1874 760 | frus1872p1 455 | frus1871 397 | frus1875v02 359 | frus1876 322 | frus1875v01 290 …
```

Measured: of **7,207** in-scope documents matching stem `fish`, **5,634 (78%) contain no fishing/fishery phrase at all** (set difference against `fisheri OR "fishing vessel" OR "fishing rights" OR "fishing boat" OR "fishing grounds" OR "fishing fleet" OR "fishing craft" OR "fishing industry" OR "fishing zone" OR "fishing limits"`). Every one of the top twelve `fish` volumes is 1870–1877.

This bit me live: a probe for `"fishing" AND (Ecuador OR Peru) AND (seizur OR arrest)` returned 116 docs across 47 volumes led by `frus1865p1`, `frus1870`, `frus1872p1`, `frus1874` — Grant-administration correspondence signed by Fish. Re-run as `(Ecuador OR Peru OR Chile) AND ("fishing vessel" OR "fishing boat" OR "tuna boat" OR fisheri) AND (seizur OR arrest OR detain OR fine)` it returns **96 docs / 38 volumes** and the 1870s volumes drop out entirely.

**Use `fisheri` (= fishery/fisheries) and explicit two-word phrases. Never the bare stem `fish`.**

Two smaller false friends found the same way: bare `"twelve mile"` and `"200 mile"` are often plain distances (the twelve-mile probe pulled in `frus1862`, `frus1864p1`, `frus1866p2`), and case-insensitive `\bLOS\b` on the TEI is swamped by Spanish *los* — see §5.

I used this expression as the "core" scope. It is deliberately built from the editors' vocabulary, not mine:

```
fisheri OR "fishing rights" OR "fishing vessel" OR "fishing vessels" OR "fishing boat"
OR "fishing grounds" OR "fishing fleet" OR "fishing craft" OR "fishing zone" OR "fishing limits"
OR "territorial waters" OR "territorial sea" OR "law of the sea" OR "continental shelf"
OR "pelagic sealing" OR "fur seal" OR "three mile limit" OR "twelve mile limit"
OR "maritime jurisdiction" OR "marine league"
```

SELECT COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT f.volume_id)
FROM frus_documents f
JOIN document_cache dc ON dc.volume_id=f.volume_id AND dc.document_id=f.document_id

**Findings the split forces:**

1. **The apparatus carries a real share.** Body text is 86.5% of characters, but `fisheries` loses 22% when notes are removed (5,832 → 4,528), `law of the sea` loses 27% (2,311 → 1,676), `pelagic sealing` loses 28% (372 → 267). Any density you publish must say which surface it is on. The SQLite index cannot make this cut at all — `body_text` contains footnotes.
2. **Singular vs plural is not cosmetic.** `territorial sea` (1,303 in 71 volumes) is a twentieth-century legal term of art; `territorial waters` (3,757 in 346 volumes) is the older general phrase. They are the *same* stem-neighbourhood but not the same concept, and the index cannot tell them apart from a stem lookup alone. Searching only one loses a whole register.
3. **Acronyms beat spelled forms in the modern band, as the house rule predicts.** Case-sensitive, on raw TEI: `EEZ` **117 occurrences in 3 volumes** vs `exclusive economic zone` 94 in 11. `IWC` (International Whaling Commission) **505 in 9 volumes**. `UNCLOS` 88 in 4 (`frus1969-76v16`, `frus1969-76ve01`, `frus1969-76ve03`, `frus1981-88v41`). `ICNAF` 7 / 2, `INPFC` 4 / 1, `IATTC` 1 / 1, `FCMA` **0**.
4. **`LOS` is a trap.** Case-insensitive `\bLOS\b` returns 3,908 occurrences in 339 volumes — Spanish *los*. Case-sensitive uppercase drops it to 816 in 40 volumes, and even that is contaminated by all-caps 19th-century headings (`frus1864p4`, `frus1868p2`, `frus1884`, `frus1886`). The genuine usage is concentrated: `frus1981-88v41` 358, `frus1969-76ve03` 183. Only use `LOS` case-sensitively and only in post-1960 volumes.
5. A negative built from modern vocabulary would be circular here, and the scan shows why: `exclusive economic zone` reaches 11 volumes and `EEZ` 3, while `marine league` (43 volumes), `three marine miles` (26) and `marginal sea` (16) carry the same argument in nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century language. **The corpus does not lack the concept before 1960; it lacks the word.**

## 7. What I would search for

**A. Follow the compilation chain, don't keyword-search it.** `frus1951v01` → `frus1952-54v01p2` → `frus1955-57v11` → `frus1958-60v02` → `frus1961-63v25` → `frus1969-76ve01` → `frus1969-76ve03` → `frus1981-88v41`, all under the heading "United States policy regarding the law of the sea" / "Law of the Sea".
