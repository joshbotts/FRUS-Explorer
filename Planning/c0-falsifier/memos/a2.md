# Scoping memo — the Department of State and international narcotics control before 1970

**Prepared from:** the SQLite copy of the FRUS Explorer index (`frus-copy.db`), the TEI corpus at
`/Users/jbotts/Development/frus/volumes/`, and the bundled JSON reference data in
`/Applications/FRUS Explorer.app/Contents/Resources/`. Scoping pass, not a finished argument.

---

## Bottom line, before the evidence

1. **The corpus holds a great deal, and it is unusually easy to find** — because the FRUS editors gave
   this subject a *named, recurring compilation* for four decades. 53 chapter headings in 40 volumes;
   1,077 pre-1970 documents mention the vocabulary, 615 of them more than once, across 124 volumes.
2. **Your phrasing will fail.** "International narcotics control" and "drug abuse" are Nixon-era terms
   (1 of 116 `drug abuse` documents is pre-1970). The corpus says *opium traffic*, *traffic in opium*,
   *narcotic drugs*, *habit-forming drugs*. And `opium` outnumbers `narcotic(s)` **4.6 : 1**.
3. **"Department of State" is a dead term here** — it is *less* frequent in the narcotics volumes
   (24.5%) than in the corpus at large (30.5%). Find the Department through its files and its
   delegations, not its name.
4. **The shape of the record is: continuous 1880–1947, then a hole in the 1950s, then a restart.**
   The 1950s yield 13 documents at the same threshold that yields 87 for the 1930s. That is a fact about
   FRUS's compilation policy, not about the Department — the work moved to the UN Commission on
   Narcotic Drugs with Treasury's Federal Bureau of Narcotics in the lead, a channel FRUS's country
   volumes do not cover.
5. **The archival key is clean and three-layered**: Numerical File **case 774** (1906–10, five digitized
   M862 rolls), then decimal **`511.4*`** (conferences), **`500.C1197`** (League Opium Advisory
   Committee) and country **`.114`** (1910–49), then **`341.9`** and **`SOC 11–5`**. One trap:
   `.114` means "liquors *and* habit-forming drugs", and `811.114` — the single largest file — is
   Prohibition, not narcotics.
6. **Two archival channels, and one of them is empty.** 498 of 615 documents say where they came from;
   only 20 documents' footnotes point anywhere at all (29 references). Do not build on the second.

## 1. Controls

Run in one pass, per house rule:

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id) FROM frus_documents
WHERE frus_documents MATCH '"Department of State"';   -- 98499 docs | 551 volumes
SELECT COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id) FROM frus_documents
WHERE frus_documents MATCH 'ZZZ_IMPOSSIBLE_ZZZ';      -- 0 | 0
```

Positive control fires in 551 of 552 volumes; negative control returns zero. The scan works.
(One volume in 552 never says "Department of State" — worth knowing if you ever use that string as a
denominator.)

## 2. False-friend test — **"Department of State" is unusable as a search term here**

The question supplied the phrase, so it gets tested:

```sql
-- corpus baseline
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM frus_documents f JOIN document_cache dc USING(volume_id, document_id)
WHERE f.frus_documents MATCH '"Department of State"'
  AND dc.is_front_matter=0 AND dc.is_editorial_note=0
  AND dc.volume_id NOT IN ('frus1951-54IranEd2','frus1969-76ve15p2Ed2');      -- 93418
-- same, restricted to 11 volumes carrying a narcotics compilation
--   (frus1908, frus1909, frus1910, frus1912, frus1923v01, frus1924v01,
--    frus1931v01, frus1934v03, frus1936v04, frus1944v02, frus1961-63v25)     -- 2355 of 9610
```

**93,418 of 306,619** corpus-wide (30.5%) against **2,355 of 9,610** in the narcotics volumes (24.5%).
The phrase is *below* baseline in the on-topic volumes. It measures the corpus, not the question, and
I stopped using it. The Department's presence in this material has to be found through its
**organisational handles** — delegation reports, bureau and office files, the decimal classes it filed
under — not through its name.

## 6. Archival channels — **two numbers, never one**

House rule: label the channel, never sum. Both are computed over the same scope, the **615 pre-1970
documents at ≥2 occurrences**.

### 6a. CAME-FROM (`document_sources`, one row per document — where FRUS got the document)

**498 of 615** carry a source row.

```sql
SELECT repository, record_group, citation_era, decimal_class, lot_file_norm, series_name
FROM document_sources WHERE volume_id=? AND document_id=?;   -- per document, aggregated in Python
```

- `citation_era` (a citation **form**, not a date): decimal 420, structured 48, published 12, lot_file 12, unrecognized 5, named_series 1.
- repository: Department of State 432, National Archives 30, Nixon Presidential Materials 13, Eisenhower Library 3, Kennedy Library 1, University of Montana 1.
- record group: RG 59 in 456 of the 498 (426 written "RG-59" + 30 written "59"); RG 256 in 6.
- lot files: only 12 rows, top being 60 D 224 (7).

**This question is a Record Group 59 central-file question.** **456 of the 498** located documents name
RG 59, and **420 of 498** carry a decimal-file citation; lot files appear on 12. Everything in §7 follows
from that.

### 6b. POINTED-AT (`external_citations`, many rows per document — what the footnotes mention)

Over the same 615 documents: **29 footnote references from 20 documents.** Repository: Department of
State 28, Nixon Presidential Materials 1. Collections: `NSC Files` (1). Lots: `54D423` (2).

This channel is effectively empty for your period and **you should not build anything on it**. The
reason is structural: FRUS's pre-1945 volumes put their archival citation in the document's own source
note (channel 6a), not in an editor's footnote. The corpus-wide decimal-class view of the pointed-at
channel confirms it — the largest narcotics-class footnote citation is `033.1140` with 5 references.

## 7. The archival key — this is the part worth keeping

The offline stack resolves the pre-1970 record in three successive filing systems. All of the following
came out of FRUS's own source notes, which is why they are trustworthy: the Department told you where it
filed the paper.

### 7b. 1910–1949 — the decimal file, three families

Verified against `decimal-class-labels.json` (the Department's own 1910–1949 schedule, the only schedule
that ships):

1. **`511.4*` — Congresses and Conferences (class 5), the opium conferences and conventions.**
   165 documents in the corpus. Sub-designators actually used in source notes: `511.4A1` (Hague/Shanghai),
   `511.4A2`, `511.4A2A` (Geneva 1924–25), `511.4A5`, `511.4A6`, `511.4-A`, `511.4R1`, `511.4C1`.
   Examples: `frus1923v01/d35` cites `511.4 A 1/1777a`; `frus1924v01/d43` cites `511.4A2/31b`;
   `frus1944v02/d907` cites `511.4A5/8–2144`.
2. **`500.C1197` — the League of Nations Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium.** (`500.C` is the League;
   `1197` is that committee.) 33 documents across 7 volumes: frus1938v04 (10), frus1934v03 (9),
   frus1935v01 (6), frus1928v01 (3), frus1934v01 (3), frus1935v03 (1), frus1939v04 (1).
3. **Country `.114` — the schedule reads, verbatim, "Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs".**
   FRUS often disambiguates it in the source note itself as `893.114 Narcotics/…` — 58 source notes carry
   that explicit sub-designator, across frus1944v02 (12), frus1936v04 (10), frus1934v03 (7), frus1935v03 (7),
   frus1937v04 (5), frus1925v02 (4), frus1924v02 (3), frus1939v04 (3), frus1947v08 (3), frus1945v02 (2),
   frus1930v02 (1), frus1944v06 (1).
   Country numbers here: 893 = China, 890 = Asia/Far East, 891 = Iran, 892 = Thailand, 812 = Mexico,
   845 = (India), 883 = (Ethiopia), 800 = general.

> **⚠ The `.114` trap — the biggest methodological hazard on this question.**
> `.114` is a **compound** class: liquors *and* habit-forming drugs. So the largest `.114` file in the
> corpus is not about narcotics at all.
>
> ```
> class            documents   with narcotics vocabulary
> 811.114 (US)          268            7          ← Prohibition-era LIQUOR
> 511.4*                165          130
> 893.114 (China)        46           44
> 033.1140               41            0
> 367.114M69             37            0
> 890.114 (Far East)     10           10
> 891.114 (Iran)          6            6
> 812.114 (Mexico)        3            3
> ```
>
> An archival-first search on `.114` returns 616 documents, of which **283 mention no narcotics vocabulary
> at all and 114 are visibly about liquor/Prohibition**. Use the *foreign-country* `.114` classes and
> `511.4*`; treat `811.114` as a separate (liquor) file unless a document says otherwise.

## 8. A corpus-scoping negative is not a research negative

Two places where FRUS stops and the record does not:

1. **The 1950s.** Thirteen documents (§5). The obvious next series is not in RG 59 at all: narcotics
   enforcement and the U.S. seat on the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs ran through the **Federal Bureau of
   Narcotics, Department of the Treasury**. `frus1969-76ve03`'s own source list names **Record Group 170,
   Records of the Drug Enforcement Administration** (which absorbed the FBN's records) — and **RG 170 appears
   nowhere in any bundled index**, so this toolset cannot take you further than the name. The multilateral
   side sits with the **League of Nations Opium Advisory Committee** and its UN successor, whose records are
   in Geneva/UN custody, not NARA at all. Note that FRUS itself keeps pointing at Treasury: the only
   narcotics figure the index tags as a person is *"Anslinger, Harvey J. — Commissioner of Narcotics,
   Department of the Treasury"*.
2. **The Turkey file of 1952–54.** §4: the editors named it and printed nothing. `891.114` / `867.114`-family
   central-file material and the Ankara embassy post files are where that goes next.

corpus-wide body/apparatus split.**

## 10. Spelling-variant split (TEI, corpus-wide)

I ran the variant scan on the **TEI**, because this database structurally cannot do it: FTS5's porter
tokenizer folds `cocaine`→`cocain` and `morphine`→`morphin`, so `MATCH 'cocain'` and `MATCH 'cocaine'`
both return exactly 105 documents and `morphin`/`morphine` both return 102 — the index cannot distinguish
the spellings at all.

**Scope: the 37 volumes carrying a narcotics compilation** (§4's list, false friends dropped). Not the
whole corpus — a corpus-wide pass over 3.1 GB of TEI was still running when I wrote this, and the
compilation set is the scope the split actually matters in.

Four things follow, and three of them change what you should search for.

1. **`opium` is the word.** 4,885 occurrences against 1,062 for `narcotic`+`narcotics` combined — 4.6:1.
   A search built on "narcotics" finds roughly a fifth of this material.
2. **The acronyms are all zero.** The house rule warns that acronyms usually outnumber spelled forms;
   here the opposite holds absolutely. CND, BNDD, INCB, PCOB, S/NM and FBN are a **post-1969** vocabulary
   (they appear in the corpus only in the 1969–76 volumes' abbreviation lists — see the `terms` table),
   which independently corroborates §3's anachronism finding. Do not search for them before 1970.
3. **The old spellings are not there.** `cocain`, `morphin`, `codein`, `hasheesh` all return zero; only
   the derived `cocainism`/`morphinism` appear. So the FTS residue in §5 is a *stemming* artifact, not a
   spelling one — worth knowing because it means your period's printed spelling is the modern one.
4. **The cannabis vocabulary is a real gap in my own scan and probably in yours.** `marihuana` (1) and
   `marijuana` (2) are effectively absent, but `cannabis` (19) and **`Indian hemp` (17 occurrences in 6
   volumes)** are not — and `Indian hemp` is the term the 1925 Geneva Convention actually uses. My §5
   density set does **not** contain the `cannabis`/`Indian hemp` documents that lack other narcotics
   vocabulary. Corpus-wide in the index, `cannabis` is 18 docs / 10 vols and `"Indian hemp"` 12 docs /
   7 vols; add both before you run this for real.

## 11. The person layer is unusable for the pre-1930 half

## 12. What I would actually search for

**Vocabulary (period-correct, not yours):** `opium`, `traffic in opium`, `opium traffic`, `narcotic drugs`,
`habit-forming drugs`, `dangerous drugs`, `opium smoking`, `opium monopoly`, `illicit traffic`,
`Opium Advisory Committee`, `Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium`, `Permanent Central Opium Board`,
`Drug Supervisory Body`, `Commission on Narcotic Drugs`, `Single Convention`, `Bureau of Narcotics`,
plus **`raw opium`**, **`prepared opium`**, **`opium smoking`**, **`coca leaf`**, and — the one my own
first pass missed — **`cannabis`** and **`Indian hemp`** (the 1925 Geneva Convention's own term).
Corpus counts for these are in §13 so you can see which are worth the keystrokes.

**Do not search acronyms before 1970.** `CND` 0 pre-1970 documents, `INCB` 0, `PCOB` 0, `FBN` 0,
`BNDD` 6 (all 1969). The only pre-1970 abbreviation in the corpus for this machinery is **`Narcom`**
(UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs), and it occurs in exactly **one** document, in `frus1961-63v25`.

**Structural (better than vocabulary):** search the *volume tables of contents* for the editors' formula —
`Opium Conference`, `traffic in narcotic drugs`, `traffic in opium`, `habit-forming drugs`. That reproduces
§4's table and is far more precise than a body-text scan.

**Archival (best of the three):** search `document_sources.raw_text` for `File No. 774`, `511.4`,
`500.C1197`, `.114 Narcotics`, `341.9`, `SOC 11–5`. That finds documents on the strength of where the
Department filed them, which is the actual question — "what role did State play" is answered by which of
State's own files the paper is in, and by whose delegation reports come back.

## 15. Honest limits of this pass

- Every count is over the 552 volumes in this index. That is the whole published series, so the
  partial-library caveat is weak — but FRUS itself is a **selection**, and §8 says where it stops.
- I did **not** separate document body from editorial footnote corpus-wide (§9); five volumes only.
- **§10's variant split covers the 37 narcotics-compilation volumes, not all 552.** A corpus-wide TEI
  pass over 3.1 GB was still running when I stopped; the script is `variants2.py` and its output will
  land in `variants_out.txt`. Nothing in this memo depends on it, but the acronym and cannabis findings
  would be stronger with it.
- I did **not** verify any NARA series creator heading, and creator attribution in this stack is a decoy
  surface (56% precision measured on one route).
- The **1950s figure (13 documents) is thin enough to be a property of FRUS's compilation policy rather
  than of the archive.** Do not publish it as an absence without checking RG 170 and the UN records.
- `citation_era` in §6a is a citation **form**, not a date, and is not plotted as one anywhere here.
- The two channels in §6 are never added together; 498 came-from and 29 pointed-at are answers to
  different questions.
- Subject tags (`document_subject_refs`) are string-matched candidates and I did not use them; `subject_tag_ids`
  is always NULL and was ignored.
- I read no `summary_text` and no `note_text`.
