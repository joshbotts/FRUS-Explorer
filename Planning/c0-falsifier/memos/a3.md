## 2. The finding you should read first: your own phrase does not work

You asked about "territorial waters". In FRUS that phrase is a **false friend**, and I can show it two ways.

**(a) The company it keeps.** Of the 1,666 documents matching `"territorial waters"`:

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM frus_documents f JOIN document_cache d USING(volume_id,document_id)
WHERE frus_documents MATCH '"territorial waters" AND (<family>)'
  AND d.is_front_matter=0 AND d.is_editorial_note=0;
```

| co-occurring family | documents |
|---|---|
| all `"territorial waters"` | 1,666 |
| + fishing / fisheries / fishermen / halibut / salmon / trawler | 435 |
| + belligerent / submarine / neutrality / blockade / warship | 620 |
| + smuggling / liquor / prohibition / rum | 467 |

(The 1,666 here is without the Ed2 suppression, which is why the lift table below shows 1,664 for the same phrase; the two Ed2 volumes contribute 2 documents.)

The phrase's centre of gravity in this corpus is **wartime neutrality** (hovering warships, 1914–18) and **Prohibition-era liquor smuggling** (the twelve-mile hovering statutes and the 1924 liquor treaties), not fish.

## 3. What the editors actually called it

The single most useful thing I did was read 25,103 section headings out of `volume_structures` before writing any query. FRUS's editors use a repeating formula, and it **changes four times**:

| period | the editors' formula | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1872–1912 | *"Fisheries"*, *"Newfoundland fishery question"*, *"Protection of fur seals in Bering Sea"*, *"seizure of British sealing-vessels"* | `frus1906p1` "Newfoundland fishery question" (70 docs) |
| 1918–1944 | *"regulation of fisheries"*, *"preservation of the halibut fishery"*, *"Fraser River sockeye salmon fisheries"*, *"Representations to Japan in regard to regulation of fisheries off the coast of Alaska"* | `frus1937v04` (40 docs) |
| 1935–1948 | *"…purporting to extend the territorial waters of X beyond the three-mile limit"*, *"continental shelf"* | `frus1945v02` "Formulation of United States policy on the resources of the Continental Shelf and on coastal fisheries" (35 docs) |
| 1950–1988 | *"delimitation of inland waters and the marginal sea"* → *"Law of the Sea"* (a standing compilation title) | `frus1958-60v02` "LAW OF THE SEA" (108 docs); `frus1981-88v41` (89 + 48 docs) |
| 1969–1976 | ***"Oceans Policy"*** | `frus1969-76ve01` (113 docs) |

**"Oceans Policy" is the heading I would most likely have missed and you would too.** My first heading scan — built from every word in your question plus the obvious synonyms — returned 1,592 documents and did not contain a single one of `frus1969-76ve01`'s 113. I only found it by taking documents that mentioned `tuna` and asking what heading they sat under. If you take one procedural thing from this memo: **scan the headings for the period before you trust a vocabulary.**

Notably absent from every heading in 552 volumes: the word **"tuna"** (0 headings), and any form of **"200-mile"** (0 headings). The Peruvian and Ecuadorian tuna-boat seizures — probably the single best-known US fisheries dispute of the twentieth century — are filed under the *country* (`Peru`, `Ecuador`, `Brazil`, `Mexico`) in the 1969–76 American Republics volumes, never under a fisheries heading. A heading-only scope misses them entirely.

Three things in that table are load-bearing:

1. **The acronym trap is real and it bit me.** A case-*insensitive* `\bLOS\b` returns **3,911** occurrences in 340 volumes. Case-sensitive it returns **816** in 40. **79.1% of the case-insensitive hits are the Spanish article *los*** — FRUS prints Spanish-language enclosures throughout the American Republics volumes. Any acronym scan on this corpus must be case-sensitive, and I would extend that suspicion to any three-letter acronym you try.
2. **Acronyms do *not* outnumber spelled forms here**, contrary to the usual pattern: `law of the sea` 2,258 vs `LOS` 816 vs `UNCLOS` 88; `exclusive economic zone` 87 vs `EEZ` 117 (the one place the acronym wins, and both are tiny). This corpus's editorial prose spells things out.
3. **`marginal sea` (77 occurrences, 10 volumes) is the period term you would never guess.** It is what the 1949–52 Department called the territorial sea, and it is the heading of `frus1950v01` "I. Delimitation of inland waters and the marginal sea". A modern-vocabulary scan finds none of it.

**Apparatus caveat, stated rather than solved:** these TEI counts are over the whole file — front matter, abbreviation lists, editorial footnotes and the index included. I did **not** separate document text from apparatus for this table. The SQL sets in §4 and §7 *do* exclude front matter and editorial notes by column, but even there `body_text` contains editorial footnotes, so those term frequencies blend the diplomats' language with the editors'.

**A scoping negative is not a research negative, so here is the series that does.** `central-files-index.json` carries a `countrySeries` block of 6,654 rolls across 12 pre-1910 State Department series, and the relevant ones are populated:

- **Diplomatic Despatches, Great Britain — 200 rolls**, "Despatches from U.S. Ministers to Great Britain, 1791–1906" (e.g. Volume 213, Oct 5 1905 – Jan 31 1906, NAID 188600418). - Diplomatic Despatches also: Russia 66, Japan 84, Mexico 179, Peru 66, Ecuador 19, Chile 52, Denmark 28, Norway 28.

It is a fact about the archive that changes where you look.

One trap: **St. John's appears under three spellings** in the geoKeys — `st. john's` (9), `st. johns` (3), `st.john` (10). A single-spelling query gets a third of the rolls and no warning.

## 9. What I would search for

In descending order of expected yield.

1. **Heading-first, always.** Take the 73 volumes in `volume_appendix.txt` and read the chapter headings. 4. **`"Oceans Policy"`** as a heading, for 1969–1976.
5. **Country + fisheries pairs** for the material outside fisheries headings: Peru, Ecuador, Brazil, Mexico, Japan, Iceland, USSR. `"Peru" AND "fishing"` = 346 documents in 76 volumes; `"Ecuador" AND "fishing"` = 231 in 59; `"Japanese fishing"` = 140 in 41; `"Iceland" AND "fishing"` = 149 in 48.

## 10. Things I am not confident about

- The heading set is my regex, not the editors' category. Two headings changed the answer materially when I found them ("Oceans Policy", +113 documents; "access to the resources of the sea"). There are probably more I did not find, and the honest statement is that **1,693 is a floor, not a count**.
- I have not separated footnote text from document text anywhere. Every term frequency here blends the diplomats with the editors.
- I glossed `842.628` as Canada and `811b.628` as a US territory by inference; the shipped country table does not contain either number.
- `person_rollup` and `person_mentions` I did not use at all. Name-based clustering under-merges, TEI tagging is uneven, and a bare-surname scan is banned — for a question whose principals are Bayard, Blaine, Gresham, Root, Hull and Stevenson, spread over a century, I did not think a lower bound would earn its place in a scoping memo.
- The lift test in §2 uses four control phrases. Four is enough to see a 1.42–1.55 band; it is not enough to put an error bar on it.
