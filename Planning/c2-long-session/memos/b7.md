# Scoping memo — fishing rights and territorial waters in FRUS

**To:** the historian
**Question as posed:** How did the United States handle disputes over fishing rights and
territorial waters with other governments?
**Status:** scoping pass. Nothing below is a finding about the history; everything below is a
finding about where the evidence is and how badly a naive search would go wrong.

---

## 0. Coverage, first, as always

```sql
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id), COUNT(*), SUM(is_front_matter), SUM(is_editorial_note)
FROM document_cache;
```
→ **552 volumes**, 316,839 rows, 1,012 front matter, 8,468 editorial notes.

The whole published series is in the index — this is not a partial-library run. Working scope

**Surfaces used.** SQLite index for every document count and the archival came-from/pointed-at

## 1. Headline: the corpus holds a lot, in two separate eras, under two different names

The short answer is that FRUS carries this subject **heavily**, but it carries it under editorial
vocabulary that changes completely in about 1950, and the modern half of it is invisible to the
phrasing your question uses.

Best current estimate of the on-topic material:

| set | documents | volumes | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — editor-designated chapters** | 2,108 (1,785 core + 349 adjacent) | 86 | documents inside a chapter the editors themselves headed as fisheries / territorial waters / law of the sea. Highest precision. |
| **B — State's own file classes** | 228 | 27 | documents whose printed source note carries decimal suffix `.0145` or `.628`. Independent of A, near-total precision. |
| **C — full-text term union** | 4,206 | 458 | any of nine phrases anywhere in the document. High recall, poor precision. |
| **A ∪ B ∪ C** | **5,180** | 458 | the outer bound worth triaging. |

Overlaps: A∩B = 126, A∩C = 1,207, B∩C = 135. **901 of A's 2,108 documents (43%) contain none of
the nine search terms**, and 102 of B's 228 sit under no maritime heading. No single channel
finds it.

## 2. What I would search for — and it is not what you asked for

### 2a. Read the editors first. Their formula changes twice.

I flattened every volume's `volume_structures` into 25,103 section nodes (all 552 volumes have a
structure; 21,163 nodes are chapter / subchapter / compilation / section) and scanned the
**editors' own headings**. Three regimes:

**Regime 1, 1872–c.1945 — "fisheries".** The heading is a bilateral dispute, named by species,
place or treaty:

- `frus1880` ch9 — *Correspondence with the Government of Great Britain in regard to the Alleged
  Outrage upon American Fishermen at Fortune Bay, Newfoundland* (16 docs)
- `frus1888p2` ch14 — *Papers relating to the seizure of British sealing-vessels in Bering Sea* (68)
- `frus1894app1` comp2 — *II. Enforcement of regulations respecting fur seals* (**144 — the single
  largest on-topic chapter in the series**)
- `frus1906p1` ch106 — *Newfoundland fishery question* (70)
- `frus1910` ch58 — *The North Atlantic Coast Fisheries Arbitration* (1)
- `frus1923v01` ch24 — *…convention…for the preservation of the halibut fishery in the Northern
  Pacific* (16)
- `frus1937v04` ch20 — *Representations to Japan in regard to regulation of fisheries off the coast
  of Alaska* (40), continued at `frus1939v02` ch10 (37)

**Regime 2, 1945–1950 — the pivot.** The vocabulary turns legal and general:

- `frus1945v02` comp21 — *Formulation of United States policy on the resources of the Continental
  Shelf and on coastal fisheries* (35)
- `frus1948v09` ch14 — *Policies of the United States and certain other American Republics with
  respect to the continental shelf and high seas fisheries* (4)
- `frus1950v01` ch6 — *I. Delimitation of inland waters and the marginal sea* (6)

**Regime 3, 1952–present — "Law of the Sea", full stop.** A standing compilation heading:

| volume | section | docs | heading |
|---|---|---|---|
| `frus1952-54v01p2` | comp5 | 28 | United States policy regarding the law of the sea |
| `frus1955-57v11` | comp2 | 29 | United States Policy Regarding the Law of the Sea |
| `frus1958-60v02` | ch8 | **108** | LAW OF THE SEA |
| `frus1961-63v25` | ch16 | 20 | Law of the Sea |
| `frus1969-76ve03` | ch1 | 54 | Law of the Sea |
| `frus1981-88v41` | ch3 | **89** | Law of the Sea |
| `frus1981-88v41` | ch6 | 48 | International Regulation of Whaling Practices |

**So: do not search for "fishing rights". Search for the editors' three formulas —
`fisher*`, `continental shelf`/`marginal sea`, and `law of the sea` — and expect them to be
mutually exclusive by period.**

### 2b. The decisive instrument nobody would guess: State's own file numbers

The bundled `decimal-class-labels.json` carries the Department's 1910–49 classification schedule.
Two subject suffixes are the question, verbatim, in the Department's own words:

- **`.0145` — "Territorial waters. Marginal sea"**
- **`.628` — "Fisheries"**

These compose with a country number (`8` = Internal Affairs of States, `94` = Japan → `894.628` =
*Japan — Fisheries*). Because `document_sources.decimal_class` stores the printed file number, the
index can be asked what the Department itself filed as a fisheries or territorial-waters matter,
regardless of what chapter it later landed in:

```sql
SELECT s.decimal_class, COUNT(*) n, COUNT(DISTINCT s.volume_id) v
FROM document_sources s JOIN document_cache d USING(volume_id,document_id)
WHERE d.is_front_matter=0 AND d.is_editorial_note=0
  AND (s.decimal_class LIKE '%.0145%' OR s.decimal_class LIKE '%.628%')
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY n DESC;
```

| class | docs | vols | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 894.628 | 71 | 8 | Japan — Fisheries |
| 890.0145 | 34 | 2 | Far East — Territorial waters. Marginal sea |
| 811.0145 | 33 | 1 | United States — Territorial waters. Marginal sea |
| 861.0145 | 21 | 1 | USSR — Territorial waters. Marginal sea |
| 812.0145 | 10 | 3 | Mexico — Territorial waters |
| 702.6282 | 10 | 1 | (relations class) |
| 812.628 | 9 | 2 | Mexico — Fisheries |
| plus 14 more keys | 40 | | |
| **total** | **228** | **27** | |

228 documents, of which **102 sit under no maritime chapter heading at all**. This is a small,
sharp, entirely independent probe and I would run it first on any follow-up.

**Stemming traps, measured against `frus_documents_vocab`.** Porter gives `fisheries`→`fisheri` but
`fishing`→`fish`; these are *different index terms* (`fisheri` 1,969 docs, `fish` 7,283). And
`fishermen` does **not** fold to `fisherman` — they are separate stems (551 and 128 docs). Querying
one form finds a fraction of the material. The index cannot do a spelling-variant count properly;
that is a TEI job.

**Spelling variants matter here more than usual.** `"Behring Sea"` (the nineteenth-century
spelling) is 216 documents across 16 volumes that `"Bering Sea"` does not return. The editors use
both, sometimes in adjacent chapters of the same volume (`frus1888p2` heads Supplement C
"Behring Sea" and its chapter "Bering Sea").

**Period vocabulary check.** Before calling anything absent from the early corpus I checked terms
contemporaries used rather than ours: `"three marine miles"` 50 docs / 26 vols, `"marine league"`
71 / 43, `"cannon shot"` 82 / 49, `"inshore fisheries"` 31 / 12, `"headland"` 58 / 39,
`"bays and harbors"` 27 / 13, `"reciprocity treaty"` 273 / 85, `"Halifax award"` 8 / 7. The
nineteenth-century limits-of-the-sea vocabulary is present. No negative here rests on modern words.

## 3. The false-friend test — and your question's own phrase fails it

House rule: for any term the question supplied, compare its rate in the on-topic volumes against
a control's. Denominators: 76,936 documents in the 94 volumes with a maritime heading, 229,683 in
the rest.

| phrase | on-topic docs | per 1,000 | elsewhere | per 1,000 | lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CONTROL `"Department of State"` | 16,178 | 210.28 | 77,240 | 336.29 | **0.6** |
| CONTROL `"the Government"` | 19,487 | 253.29 | 48,920 | 212.99 | **1.2** |
| CONTROL `"foreign minister"` | 3,478 | 45.21 | 22,321 | 97.18 | **0.5** |
| `"territorial waters"` | 643 | 8.36 | 1,021 | 4.45 | 1.9 |
| `"fishing rights"` | 82 | 1.07 | 124 | 0.54 | 2.0 |
| `"law of the sea"` | 264 | 3.43 | 395 | 1.72 | 2.0 |
| `"high seas"` | 631 | 8.20 | 1,013 | 4.41 | 1.9 |
| `fisheri` | 935 | 12.15 | 952 | 4.14 | 2.9 |
| `"three mile limit"` | 157 | 2.04 | 83 | 0.36 | **5.6** |
| `"fur seal"` | 244 | 3.17 | 55 | 0.24 | **13.2** |

The control band is 0.5–1.2. **`"territorial waters"` sits at 1.9 — barely above it.** 1,021 of its
1,664 hits, 61%, are in volumes with no maritime chapter at all. I sampled fourteen of those at
random and read only their headers: `frus1919Parisv13/ch12subch8` (*Section VIII.—Poland, Art. 87
to 93*), `frus1946v04/d41` (*Proposals of Amendment to the Military Clauses*),
`frus1961-63v01/d149` (Chief of Naval Operations to Secretary of Defense), `frus1890/d261`
(Salisbury to Edwardes), `frus1969-76v05/d443` (NSC staff memorandum). This is ordinary diplomatic
and naval prose.

**Conclusion: `"territorial waters"` is close to measuring the corpus rather than the question, and
`"fishing rights"` (206 documents in the entire corpus) is too rare to scope with.** Both of the
phrases your question supplied are the wrong instrument. `"fur seal"` at 13.2 and `"three mile
limit"` at 5.6 are the ones that discriminate.

One more false friend worth recording because it nearly bit: scanning headings for a liquor-and-
smuggling family, a bare substring `rum` returns **154 "Rumania"/"Roumania" headings**. As a
whole word (`\brum\b`) it returns 0. Substring matching on this corpus is not safe.

**The subject runs against the grain of the series.** FRUS peaks in the 1940s (74,043 documents);
this question peaks in the 1890s and again in the 1970s–80s, and is at its thinnest exactly where
the corpus is thickest. A researcher who goes where the documents are will go to the wrong decades.

Two regimes, cleanly separated by the world wars:
- **1880–1910:** the Bering Sea fur-seal arbitration, the Newfoundland/North Atlantic fisheries, the
  Fortune Bay claims. Bilateral, arbitral, Anglo-American.
- **1945–1988:** continental shelf, 200-mile claims, UNCLOS. Multilateral, legal, and increasingly
  a Latin American story.

**The trough is partly an artefact of my method.** In the A set the 1960s show 61 documents. But
`tuna` returns 156 documents across 52 volumes (70 of them in the 1970s) and `"200-mile"` returns
376 across 159 — **and not one chapter heading in the entire corpus contains "tuna" or "shrimp"**
(0 of 21,163 headings, against a positive control of 97). The editors filed the Peru/Ecuador tuna-boat
seizures inside *country* chapters, not under a fisheries head. Top volumes for `tuna`:
`frus1969-76ve01` (20), `frus1969-76ve10` (18), `frus1969-76ve03` (15), `frus1981-88v41` (8).
Caution: `"200-mile"` also returns 6 documents dated to the 1860s, which are literal distances —
that phrase is not a clean marker before 1945.

### Channel: POINTED-AT (`external_citations`, many rows per document)

**This channel barely exists for this question, and that is itself the finding.**

Over the A set: **111 citation rows from 91 of 2,108 documents.** Repository: Department of State
81, Reagan Library 30 — and nothing else. Collections: `Guhin` 17, `Meese Files` 3, `Cobb` 3,
`Papers of George P. Shultz` 2. Lots: 94D419 (6), 86D362 (5), 94D542 (2), 92D622 (2), 85D105 (2).

The reason is structural: footnote citations to lot files and presidential libraries are a
post-1945 editorial practice, and this subject's centre of mass is the 1880s–1920s. **Every one of
those 111 pointers comes from the modern end of the material.** The two channels are two different
numbers over one scope and must never be added.

be false. It is an absence in the tooling.

### A methodological warning about the bundled usage index

I ran `collection-usage-index.json` over an 18-volume core scope (volumes where maritime chapters
are ≥25 documents *and* ≥5% of the volume). The top results came back
`740.00119` (372), `763.72` (230), `861.00` (200) — the Second World War, the First World War, and
Russian internal affairs. **That index is volume-grain, and this subject lives in chapters inside
volumes about something else.** `frus1916Supp` is 92 on-topic documents out of 1,316; `frus1939v02`
is 48 of 885. For a chapter-scale question the volume-scoped bundled index is the wrong instrument
and `document_sources` at document grain is the right one. I report this because a plausible-looking
archival ranking here would have been entirely spurious.

## 7. What I would do next, in order

1. **Run the `.0145` / `.628` probe first** and read the 102 documents it finds that no chapter
   heading covers. Smallest, sharpest, cheapest.
2. **Take the seven Law of the Sea compilations as a block** (`frus1952-54v01p2` comp5 →
   `frus1981-88v41` ch3, 328 documents). That is the modern policy story, already assembled by
   the editors, and it is invisible to the phrase "fishing rights".
3. **Take `frus1894app1` comp2 (144 docs) and `frus1888p2` ch14 (68)** as the nineteenth-century
   block. Bering Sea fur seals is the largest single episode in the corpus on this question.
4. **Chase the tuna/200-mile material by country, not by subject** — `frus1969-76ve01`, `ve03`,
   `ve10` and `frus1981-88v41`. The editors did not head it; the index and the file numbers find it.
5. **Archival:** request RG 59 lot 68D47 (NAID 1935994, entry A1 3071), the Legal Adviser's Special
   Functional Problems files 1944–1963. For the pre-1940 half, the decimal classes (`894.628`,
   `811.0145`, `890.0145`, `861.0145`, `812.0145`) are the request, not a series NAID — the bundled
   stack cannot reach that far back.
6. **[TEI, not done here]** Any density claim — how much of a chapter is fishing versus footnote —
   needs the XML. `body_text` in this index contains editorial footnotes, so every frequency above
   blends the editors' voice with the documents'. And a proper spelling-variant count
   (Bering/Behring, fishery/fisheries/fishing, three-mile/three marine miles/marine league) cannot
   be done in SQL, because porter stemming has already folded some of the distinctions away.

## 8. Caveats, collected

- All 552 volumes present; no partial-library caveat applies. Counts exclude 1,012 front-matter and
  8,468 editorial-note rows and the two duplicate second editions.
- `body_text` includes editorial footnotes. Every FTS count blends document and editor language.
- The three candidate sets are candidates. I read chapter headings and a fourteen-document sample of
  headers; **I did not read a single document body**, and no claim above rests on one.
- `citation_era` is a citation *form*, not a date; the "decimal 1,117" row above says how those
  documents were cited, not when they were written.
- Came-from and pointed-at are two channels over one scope. 1,349 source notes and 111 citation
  rows describe different things and are never summed.
- Person tables were not used. They cover 287 of 552 volumes and are near-empty before 1930, which
  is exactly this subject's first peak — any person-based approach here would have failed silently.
- The 1980s figures ride on 5,949 corpus documents from an unfinished subseries (12 of the 1981–88
  volumes are published). The high per-1,000 rate there is real; the absolute counts will grow.
