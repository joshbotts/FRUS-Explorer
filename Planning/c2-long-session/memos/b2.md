# Scoping memo — The Department of State and international narcotics control before 1970

**To:** the historian
**Question as posed:** What role did the Department of State play in international narcotics control before 1970?
**Status:** scoping pass. Nothing below is an argument about what the Department *did*; it is
an account of what this corpus holds, how I found it, and where the archive continues past
the printed page.

---

## 0. Coverage, scope, and controls

```sql
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id), COUNT(*), SUM(is_front_matter), SUM(is_editorial_note)
FROM document_cache;
```
**552 volumes · 316,839 documents · 1,012 front matter · 8,468 editorial notes.**
Your library holds the complete 552-volume series, so nothing here is limited by a partial
download. It remains a snapshot of a live series: §6 shows where that matters.

Working scope for every count below — apparatus excluded, duplicate second editions
suppressed (`frus1951-54IranEd2`, `frus1969-76ve15p2Ed2`; `frus1977-80v09Ed2` kept, it has
no first edition):

```sql
... WHERE is_front_matter=0 AND is_editorial_note=0
    AND volume_id NOT IN ('frus1951-54IranEd2','frus1969-76ve15p2Ed2')
```
→ **550 volumes, 306,619 documents**, of which **305,280 carry a parsable date**.
Periodisation is on `document_dates.date_iso` = TEI `frus:doc-dateTime-min`, the
**document's own date**, never the volume's series year.

**Scan controls, run in the same pass:**
```sql
SELECT COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id) FROM frus_documents
WHERE frus_documents MATCH '"Department of State"';   -- 98,499 hits / 551 volumes
SELECT COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT volume_id) FROM frus_documents
WHERE frus_documents MATCH 'ZZZ_IMPOSSIBLE_ZZZ';      -- 0 hits / 0 volumes
```
Positive fires in **551 of 552** volumes, negative returns zero. The one miss is
`frus1919Parisv05`, the Paris council minutes, which speak of "the American Delegation"
rather than the Department — a real absence with a documentary reason.

**Surface note.** Everything below is measured on **this index**, whose FTS is
porter-stemmed and whose `body_text` merges document prose with editorial footnotes. I did
**not** go to the TEI XML. So I make no claim about spelling-variant *frequencies* or about
a body-vs-footnote split; where that distinction would change an answer I say so.

## 1. Headline

Restricted to documents dated before 1970 and in scope, the corpus holds:

| instrument | documents | volumes |
|---|---:|---:|
| under a **narcotics chapter heading** the editors themselves wrote | **498** | 34 |
| matching a **discriminating narcotics term** | **1,072** | 212 |
| carrying a **Narcotics-family subject tag** in the bundled subject index | **1,009** | 199 |
| **CORE** — chapter heading *and* term (my recommended reading list) | **431** | 34 |

Three instruments built from three independent sources agree closely: the term set and the
subject-tag set share **970** documents, which is 970 of 1,072 and 970 of 1,009. **412 of
the 431 CORE documents** are also subject-tagged. That convergence is the main reason I
believe the scan works.

```sql
SELECT (CAST(substr(dd.date_iso,1,4) AS INTEGER)/10)*10 AS decade, COUNT(*)
FROM frus_documents f
JOIN document_cache d  ON d.volume_id=f.volume_id AND d.document_id=f.document_id
JOIN document_dates dd ON dd.volume_id=d.volume_id AND dd.document_id=d.document_id
WHERE f.frus_documents MATCH 'opium OR narcotic OR narcotics OR morphine OR cocaine
   OR "habit forming" OR poppy OR poppies OR opiate OR opiates OR marihuana OR marijuana
   OR cannabis OR hashish OR codeine'
  AND d.is_front_matter=0 AND d.is_editorial_note=0
  AND d.volume_id NOT IN ('frus1951-54IranEd2','frus1969-76ve15p2Ed2') AND dd.date_iso<>''
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
```

**Read against the corpus denominator rather than in raw counts, the printed record peaks
in the 1900s and collapses after 1940.** The 1940s and 1950s are the *thinnest* decades in
the whole series for this subject — 1.8 documents per thousand, against 16.1 in the decade
of the Shanghai Commission. The 1970s figure is there only to bound the story: the
Nixon-era drug war is a twelve-fold jump on the 1950s, and it is where the series' own
attention went.

## 2. What I searched for, and how I chose it

### 2a. I read the editors' chapter headings first

The single most productive move was not a text query. `volume_structures` holds each
volume's section tree; walking all 552 and matching section *titles* against a narcotics
vocabulary returns **53 headings**, which is very close to a table of contents for the
subject. The editors write in repeating formulas, and the formulas change by era — my
modern phrasing ("narcotics control", "drug policy") appears in none of the pre-war ones:

| era | the editors' actual formula | volumes |
|---|---|---|
| 1906–1907 | *"Restriction(s) upon the importation, growth, and use of opium"* | frus1906p1, frus1907p1 |
| 1908–1911 | *"Joint International Commission for the Investigation of the Opium Question in the Far East"* | frus1908–frus1911 |
| 1912–1915 | *"First / Second / Third International Opium Conference"* | frus1912–frus1915 |
| 1918 | *"Opium and morphine traffic in China"* | frus1918 |
| 1923 | *"…League of Nations Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium"* | frus1923v01 |
| 1924 | *"International Conference for Control of the Traffic in Habit-Forming Drugs"* | frus1924v01 |
| 1924–1925 | *"…restrict the export of opium from ports in the Persian Gulf"* | frus1924v02, frus1925v02 |
| 1926–1929 | *"…traffic in narcotic drugs"* | frus1926v01, frus1928v01, frus1929v01 |
| 1931 | *"…limiting the manufacture and regulating the distribution of narcotic drugs"* · *"Conference on the Suppression of Opium Smoking, Bangkok"* | frus1931v01 |
| 1934–1939 | *"Problem of controlling the traffic in opium and narcotic drugs in China, including Manchuria and Jehol"* | frus1934v03 … frus1939v04 |
| 1936 | *"Conference for the Suppression of the Illicit Traffic in Dangerous Drugs, Geneva"* | frus1936v01 |
| 1944–1945 | *"Initiative of the United States to secure cooperation among interested governments on measures to limit and control production and to suppress illicit traffic in opium"* | frus1944v02, frus1945v02 |
| 1947 | *"United States efforts to eliminate illicit traffic in narcotic drugs between Mexico and the United States"* | frus1947v08 |
| 1961–63 | *"Narcotics"* (one word) | frus1961-63v25 |

If you take nothing else from this memo: **the phrase to search 1924 with is "habit-forming
drugs", and the phrase to search 1908 with is "the opium question".**

### 2b. Then a term scan, stemming-aware

The FTS tokenizer is porter, so index terms are stems. Reading `frus_documents_vocab`
directly (documents containing the stem, all columns, whole database):
`traffic` 6,086 · `drug` 1,131 · `opium` 1,060 · `narcot` 871 · `poppi` 220 · `heroin` 192 ·
`cocain` 106 · `morphin` 102 · `marijuana` 29 · `opiat` 27 · `hashish` 26 · `codein` 20 ·
`marihuana` 19 · `cannabi` 18.

Note that **`marijuana` (29) outnumbers `marihuana` (19)** — both spellings are present and
either alone under-counts. I queried both.

### 2c. FALSE-FRIEND TEST — and two of the question's own words fail it

The question supplied "narcotics" and, implicitly, "drug". Testing each term's share of
documents in the 34 on-topic volumes against its share of the whole 306,619-document scope,
with four control phrases in the same pass:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| `"habit forming"` | 17 | 0.0005 | 26 | 0.0001 | **6.24** |
| `cocaine` | 54 | 0.0017 | 105 | 0.0003 | **4.91** |

| `opium` | 448 | 0.0139 | 1,040 | 0.0034 | **4.11** |
| `poppy OR poppies` | 60 | 0.0019 | 219 | 0.0007 | 2.61 |
| `narcotic*` | 182 | 0.0057 | 825 | 0.0027 | 2.10 |
| **`drug*`** | 226 | 0.0070 | 1,108 | 0.0036 | **1.95** |
| **`heroin`** | 29 | 0.0009 | 191 | 0.0006 | **1.45** |

| *CONTROL* `tariff` | 831 | 0.0259 | 6,512 | 0.0212 | *1.22* |
| *CONTROL* `telegram` | 20,226 | 0.6293 | 173,269 | 0.5651 | *1.11* |

| *CONTROL* `ZZZ_IMPOSSIBLE_ZZZ` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | *0.00* |

The controls sit at **0.74–1.42**. That is the "measuring the corpus, not the question"
band, and two terms fall in or barely above it:

- **`heroin` at 1.45 is statistically indistinguishable from the `"Secretary of State"`
  control.** Before 1970, heroin is not a discriminating word in this corpus. Of its 191
  documents corpus-wide, only 71 are pre-1970.
- **`drug*` at 1.95 is barely above the control band, and the test was rigged in its
  favour** — the on-topic volume set was itself selected by headings containing the word
  "drug". Even so it barely separates. **Do not scope on "drug" alone.**

I therefore excluded `drug*` and `heroin` from the discriminating query and used:
`opium OR narcotic OR narcotics OR morphine OR cocaine OR "habit forming" OR opiate OR
opiates OR poppy OR poppies OR marihuana OR marijuana OR cannabis OR hashish OR codeine`.

### 2d. Three false friends found and removed by reading, not by rule

The heading scan initially returned 704 documents. **61 of them are not about narcotics**,
and I only know that because I opened them:

1. **`frus1920v02/ch25subch1`, 44 documents.** Title matched on "drug". The full heading
   reads *"…American views on the delivery and distribution of **dyestuffs and chemical
   drugs** — Arrangements between the Department of State and the Textile Alliance."* This
   is the post-Armistice German dye and chemical trade. Removed.
2. **`frus1915Supp/subch15` and `frus1916Supp/subch33`, "Importation of drugs", 17
   documents.** Reading them: Herman A. Metz of Farbwerke-Hoechst, **salvarsan and
   novocaine** blocked by the British blockade — e.g. `frus1915Supp/d374` ("re salvars…"),
   `frus1916Supp/d700` ("Delay in obtaining shipments of novoc…"). Pharmaceutical
   contraband in wartime, not narcotics control. Removed.
3. **`frus1952-54v08/ch7`, 32 documents — a live anomaly you should check.** Its heading
   advertises *"AND THE PROBLEM OF ILLICIT TRAFFIC IN NARCOTICS"*, but **none of its 32
   documents contains any narcotics term**, and the whole volume contains exactly one
   (`frus1952-54v08/d577`, a Soviet Union document outside the chapter). The chapter's
   documents are Turkish economic and military aid and oil. Either the compilation was
   announced and the narcotics documents were not printed, or they sit somewhere this
   index's structure does not attach to `ch7`. **This is the one place where I would go to
   the TEI before writing anything.**

### Regime 2 — Before the chapters: 1861–1905, unindexed by the editors

**This is the part a heading-only scope loses entirely.** The earliest narcotics document in
the corpus is `frus1862/d669`, dated **1861-12-24**, Anson Burlingame to Seward — the first
year of the series. Between 1861 and 1905 there is no narcotics chapter anywhere, yet the
1880s carry the third-highest *rate* in the corpus (9.7 per thousand). The material sits in
the ordinary China correspondence:

- `frus1887/d136` (1886) — the consul-general asks whether *"the storage of opium by an
  American firm"* is forbidden under Article II of the 1880 treaty.
- `frus1887/d148` (1887) — Bayard approves Denby's letter *"in regard to American citizens
  and the opium traffic in China."*
- `frus1887/d150`, `/d163`, `/d167` — the Chefoo Convention opium tax, the Hong Kong
  commission of June 1886, and *"the necessity of opium legislation."*
- Earlier still: `frus1879/d304` (1878-09-09), `frus1879/d313`, `frus1881/d133`.

**The Department was adjudicating American participation in the opium trade twenty years
before it convened the Shanghai Commission**, and the editors gave that no heading.

A second, different pre-1906 cluster: **`frus1894app2`, 40 documents.** These are Minister
John L. Stevens on the Hawaiian *"opium ring"* and the opium-licence scandal, used as an
argument for annexation (`frus1894app2/d178`, `/d180`, `/d192`, `/d196`). Opium as domestic
political ammunition, not as control diplomacy — a different question that shares a
vocabulary, and one you should decide whether to include before it inflates a count.

### Regime 3 — The 1948–1960 dispersal

**There is no narcotics chapter anywhere between 1947 and 1961.** But the subject does not
vanish; it **changes form**, from a compilation of its own into a clause inside other
business. In the whole period 1946–1960 there are **107** narcotics-term documents, and the
volumes holding them are not narcotics volumes:

`frus1951v06p1` 10 · `frus1950v06` 9 · `frus1948v06` 9 · `frus1946v01` 6 ·
`frus1952-54v12p2` 5 · `frus1955-57v03mSupp` 4 · `frus1955-57v01` 4 · `frus1952-54v03` 4 ·
`frus1952-54v01p2` 4 … (22 volumes contribute, most of them 1–3).

What they actually are:

- **The Japanese Peace Treaty.** Japan's adherence to the narcotics conventions is a
  standing clause in the Dulles Mission drafts — `frus1950v06/d745`, `frus1951v06p1/d472`,
  `/d482`, `/d496`, and a British aide-mémoire at `frus1951v06p1/d524` citing *"The
  International Narcotics Conventions of 1912."*
- **Southeast Asian political economy.** `frus1950v06/d115`, a Department policy statement
  on Burma, *"encourage the Government of Burma to prohibit the production of opium for
  nonmedical purposes"*; `frus1950v06/d470` on the Meo, *"price opium chief money crop"*;
  `frus1951v06p1/d145` on the Thai police chief Phao and *"huge profits made… in opium"*;
  `frus1951v06p1/d232` on the Indochina opium trade.
- **Indonesia.** `frus1948v06/d225`–`/d229`, `/d234`, `/d282` — opium smuggling charges
  levelled at the Republic during the Dutch conflict.

**So the 1948–1960 "gap" is a gap in the editors' organisation, not in the archive.** If you
scope on chapters you will conclude the Department stopped; if you scope on terms you find
narcotics arriving as a treaty clause, an aid condition, and an accusation.

### Regime 4 — The return, 1961 onward

`frus1961-63v25/ch9` is titled simply **"Narcotics"**: 20 documents, all 20 narcotics-bearing,
1961–1963. Then `frus1969-76ve01/ch3` (*"U.S. Policy Towards International Production and
Trafficking in Illegal Drugs"*, 85 documents) and `frus1969-76ve03/ch4` (*"Drug Control,
1973–1976"*, 60 documents) — both outside your period, both far larger than anything before
them.

## 4. The archival channel — where the record continues past the printed page

FRUS is a selection. Two channels, **labelled and never summed**, over the CORE set of 431
pre-1970 documents in 34 volumes.

### Channel A — CAME-FROM (`document_sources`: where the printed document came from)

**411 of 431 CORE documents carry a source note.** Repository: **Department of State 384,
National Archives 17**. Record group: **RG 59, 401 of 401 attributed**. Citation *forms*
(`citation_era` is a form, not a date): decimal 383 · structured 17 · published 9 · lot_file 1
· unrecognized 1.

The file numbers resolve into a clean, three-part institutional history:

**(i) 1907–1910 — Numerical File case 774.**
```sql
SELECT ds.volume_id, COUNT(*) FROM document_sources ds JOIN document_cache dc USING(volume_id,document_id)
WHERE (ds.raw_text LIKE 'File No. 774/%' OR ds.raw_text LIKE '774/%')
  AND dc.is_front_matter=0 AND dc.is_editorial_note=0 GROUP BY 1;
```
→ `frus1907p1` 35 · `frus1908` 56 · `frus1909` 27 · `frus1910` 30 = **148 documents**.
Case 774 *is* the opium question in the 1906–1910 Numerical File.

**(ii) 1910–1949 — the decimal file, in two families.**
- **Class 5, Congresses and Conferences:** `511.4A1` (31 docs, 1910–1915), `511.4-A` (51,
  1922–1928), `511.4A6` (28, 1930–1931), `511.4C1` (7, 1932–33), `511.4A2A` (9, 1939–40),
  `511.4R1` (8, 1931), `511.4A5` (8, 1944–45). Also `500.C1197` (29) for the League's
  Advisory Committee.
- **Class 8, Internal Affairs of States, suffix `.114`.** The bundled
  `decimal-class-labels.json` glosses this exactly: class `8` = *Internal Affairs of States*,
  subject `.114` = **"Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs"**, country numbers from the
  same schedule. So `893.114` = **China — traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs** (46
  docs), `890.114` = Asia (10), `891.114` = Iran (6), `883.114` = Egypt (3), `812.114` =
  Mexico (3), `845.114` = India (2).

**(iii) 1961–1963 — class `341.9`, then subject-numeric `SOC 11`.** All 13 `341.9` documents
in the corpus are in `frus1961-63v25`. Raw notes read e.g. *"RG 59, Central Files 1960–63,
341.9/1–1961. Official Use Only. Anslinger was Commissioner of Narcotics…"*
(`frus1961-63v25/d326`). The same chapter also cites **`SOC 11–5`** and **`SOC 11–15 UN`** —
the subject-numeric class that replaced the decimal file — plus **RG 59, IO Files: Lot
67 D 378**, **Conference Files: Lot 65 D 366**, and **Kennedy Library, National Security
Files**. `SOC 11` then carries the topic forward: 19 documents in `frus1969-76ve01`, 7 in
`ve08`, 7 in `ve04`.

### **⚠ The `.114` trap — the most useful negative in this memo**

Anyone scoping this question by decimal class would reach for `*.114`. Do not, unqualified:

```sql
SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN dc.body_text LIKE '%liquor%' OR dc.body_text LIKE '%prohibition%'
                 OR dc.body_text LIKE '%rum%' OR dc.body_text LIKE '%alcohol%' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS liquor,
       SUM(CASE WHEN dc.body_text LIKE '%narcotic%' OR dc.body_text LIKE '%opium%'
                 OR dc.body_text LIKE '%morphine%' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS narcotic, COUNT(*)
FROM document_sources ds JOIN document_cache dc USING(volume_id,document_id)
WHERE ds.decimal_class='811.114' AND dc.is_front_matter=0 AND dc.is_editorial_note=0;
```
**`811.114` (country 11 = United States) holds 268 documents across 16 volumes, 1914–1938.
214 of them mention liquor or Prohibition; 7 mention narcotics.** It is the largest `.114`
class in the corpus and it is **Prohibition-era liquor smuggling**. The Department filed
liquor and habit-forming drugs in one class, and for the United States the liquor half
swamps everything.

**The Department's own disambiguator is the sub-designator.** Source notes read
`893.114 Narcotics/1460` (`frus1936v04/d596`), `890.114 Narcotics/8` (`frus1944v02/d895`),
`812.114 Narcotics/8–947` (`frus1947v08/d713`). Counting them:

```sql
SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN ds.raw_text LIKE '%.114 Narcotics%' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END),
       SUM(CASE WHEN ds.raw_text LIKE '%.114%' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
FROM document_sources ds JOIN document_cache dc USING(volume_id,document_id) WHERE ...;
```
→ **58 of 455** `.114` source notes carry the literal `Narcotics` tag, in 12 volumes spanning
1924–1947. **In the archive, ask for `893.114 Narcotics`, not `893.114`.**

### Channel B — POINTED-AT (`external_citations`: what a footnote mentions but does not print)

**Essentially unavailable for this topic, and that is itself the finding.** Over the 431
CORE documents: **9 footnote citations from 9 documents** — all Department of State, all
decimal (`341.9` ×3, `511.4A2` ×2, `500.C1197` ×2, `600.C1197`, `511.4R1`), **no lot files
and no library collections at all**. Widening to all 1,072 term hits lifts it to 75
citations from 48 documents, and only then do libraries appear (Kennedy 3, Eisenhower 1,
Johnson 1, Nixon 1) with two lot files (`54D423` ×6, `62D181`).

The two channels are **411** and **9** for the same document set. They are not comparable
and must never be added. The reason is structural: the pre-1945 volumes' footnotes cite
printed sources and decimal file numbers, not archival collections, so "what the editors
consulted and chose not to print" is a question this index cannot answer for the era where
your question sits.

### Where the bundled stack stops — measured, not assumed

- **0 of 1,065** lot files in `central-files-index.json` have a title naming narcotics,
  opium or drugs. The lot-file route reaches nothing here.
- The index carries the **1906–1910 Numerical File** and pre-1906 country series, but **no
  series NAID for the 1910–1949 decimal central file**. So `893.114 Narcotics` and `511.4A6`
  cannot be resolved offline to a NARA series — you will need the catalogue.
- `digitized-ranges-index.json` covers **class 131 only** (Visa Division reports of births).
  No range covers `511.4A6`, `893.114`, `341.9` or `812.114`. **No decimal-file digitisation
  for this subject.**

## 6. Caveats you should hold against every number above

- **Counting surface.** All figures are **document row counts on this index**, whose
  `body_text` includes editorial footnotes. A document counts once whether the word appears
  once or forty times, and a footnote mention counts as a document mention. I did not touch
  the TEI, so no figure here is a word frequency and none is apparatus-separated.
- **Porter stemming.** The index stores stems. `chiefs of mission` and `chiefs of missions`
  fold together; so would any singular/plural pair I might have wanted to distinguish. The
  spelling-variant split I *can* report (`marijuana` 29 vs `marihuana` 19) is from the vocab
  table, and is a document count over the whole database including apparatus.
- **`citation_era` is a form, not a date.** I have used it only to describe citation
  grammar, never as a timeline.
- **The two archival channels are 411 and 9.** Do not combine them; the second is thin
  because of how pre-1945 footnotes cite, not because the editors consulted nothing.
- **Subject tags are string-matched candidates.** The bundled Narcotics-family tags
  (`Narcotics` df 631, `Opium` 1,016, `Heroin` 175, `Narcotics control` 168, `Cocaine` 100,
  `Morphine` 99, `Narcotics traffic` 69, `Drug industry` 8) agree with my term scan on 970
  documents, which is why I trust the scan — but they are not semantic analysis and 39 of
  them carry no discriminating term at all.
- **The series is unfinished.** The 1981-88 subseries holds 12 volumes and 1989-92 holds 1;
  the corpus's latest coverage year is 1991. Nothing here is affected before 1970, but any
  "the Department stopped caring" reading of the post-war decline must reckon with the fact
  that the 1970s figure (456 documents, 20.5 per thousand) is the largest in the series and
  the 1980s are barely published.
- **Person coverage is thin for this era and cannot be used to rank actors.** TEI person
  markup exists in only 287 of 552 volumes and covers **zero** documents dated in the 1880s,
  1890s or 1900s — precisely the decades this question needs. Every person figure in §3 is a
  full-text phrase count, not a markup count, for that reason.
- **1,339 of 306,619 in-scope documents are undated** and are absent from every decade table.
  Separately, 1,216 chapter-container rows exist in `document_cache` with real body text;
  they are undated, so none entered the dated sets — I checked (`0` of the 1,072).
