## 1. What I searched for, and why not the words in your question

I did **not** start from "narcotics control". I started from the editors' own chapter headings,
scanning all 552 volumes' `volume_structures` for a narcotics vocabulary, then read the headings
back. That gave the period's formulae, which are nothing like the modern phrase:

|---|---|
| 1906–07 | *Restriction upon the importation, growth, and use of opium* |
| 1908–11 | *Joint International Commission for the Investigation of the Opium Question in the Far East* |
| 1912–15 | *First / Second / Third International Opium Conference* |
| 1915–16 | *Importation of drugs* |
| 1918 | *Opium and morphine traffic in China* |
| 1923 | *League of Nations Advisory Committee on Traffic in Opium* |
| 1924 | *International Conference for Control of the Traffic in **Habit-Forming Drugs*** |
| 1924–25 | *…restrict the export of opium from ports in the Persian Gulf* |
| 1926–32 | *…traffic in **narcotic drugs*** |
| 1931 | *Conference on the Suppression of Opium Smoking, Bangkok* |

**False-friend test on the words your question supplied.** Share of documents containing each term
in the 39 on-topic volumes (36,350 documents) against the whole scoped corpus (306,619):

| term (FTS stem) | on-topic volumes | corpus | enrichment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `"illicit traffic"` | 103 / 36,350 = 0.28% | 166 / 306,619 = 0.05% | **5.23×** |
| `opium` | 543 = 1.49% | 1,040 = 0.34% | **4.40×** |
| `narcot` | 311 = 0.86% | 825 = 0.27% | **3.18×** |
| `smuggl` | 344 = 0.95% | 1,557 = 0.51% | 1.86× |
| `traffic` | 1,004 = 2.76% | 5,951 = 1.94% | 1.42× |
| `treati` | 5,571 = 15.33% | 45,907 = 14.97% | 1.02× |
| `conference` | 6,430 = 17.69% | 54,116 = 17.65% | **1.00×** |
| `habit` | 165 = 0.45% | 1,724 = 0.56% | 0.81× |
| `control` | 4,149 = 11.41% | 56,513 = 18.43% | **0.62×** |
| `"Department of State"` | 8,176 = 22.49% | 93,418 = 30.47% | **0.74×** |

Three of the four content words in your question — *control*, *Department of State*, and
*conference*, the obvious next guess — are **at or below corpus baseline**. Searching on them
measures FRUS, not narcotics. `control` is actually *rarer* in the on-topic volumes than corpus-wide.
Only *opium*, *narcotic* and *illicit traffic* discriminate.

`habit` at 0.81× is the porter-stemming trap in action: the index folds `habit-forming` into the
ordinary word `habit` (1,724 documents), so the 1924 conference's own official name is
**unsearchable in this database**. That is one of the questions only the TEI can answer.

660 documents in 39 volumes carry a narcotics chapter heading. I removed one false positive by
reading the full heading: `frus1920v02/ch25subch1` matched on "chemical **drugs**" and is about

|---|---:|---:|

| 2 · League of Nations era, 1919–1933 | 111 | 10 |

| 7 · 1969 and after | 145 | 2 |

### Pass B — the words, anywhere in the corpus
```sql
frus_documents MATCH 'opium OR narcot OR heroin OR morphin OR cocain OR opiat
                      OR hashish OR marihuana OR marijuana OR cannabi'
```
**1,619 scoped documents.** Restricted to document dates before 1970: **1,154 documents in 213
volumes**.

### Pass C — the filing system, via `collection-usage-index.json`
State's own subject suffix `.114` — *Traffic in liquors and habit-forming drugs* — is a third,
independent channel. Corpus-wide: **25 distinct `.114` class keys, 448 documents, 44 volumes.**

### The three disagree, and the disagreements are the finding

| | count |
|---|---:|
| chapter documents (A) | 660 |
| …that contain a narcotics term | 575 |
| …that do **not** | **85** |
| term hits outside any narcotics chapter (B − A) | **1,044** |

So the topical and lexical channels each see something the other misses by roughly the same order of
magnitude. And Pass C finds **24 volumes carrying `.114`-filed documents that appear in no narcotics
chapter at all** — `frus1917`, `frus1919Parisv01/v02`, `frus1919Russia`, `frus1922v01`,
`frus1926v02`, `frus1928v02`, `frus1930v01/v02`, `frus1932v02`, `frus1933v03`, `frus1934v02`,
`frus1936v01/v02`, `frus1937v02`, `frus1938v02`, `frus1939v02`, `frus1940v02`, `frus1944v06`,
`frus1950v05`, `frus1950v06`, `frus1958-60v05`, `frus1958-60v10p2`, and others. Spot-checked,
these are real: `frus1933v03/d339`, `/d345`, `/d356`, `/d392` are Geneva–Mukden–Tokyo traffic filed
at `893.114-N`, in a volume with no narcotics chapter. **Start there** — they are the leads a
chapter-based scope would never surface.

**One trap inside Pass C, and it is expensive.** The largest `.114` key corpus-wide is **811.114
(United States), 268 documents in 16 volumes — and only 8 of 269 contain any narcotics term.** The
rest is Prohibition: the Federal Prohibition Commissioner, the Attorney General, the British
Ambassador, liquor smuggling. State filed *both* prohibitions in one class because the schedule
says "liquors **and** habit-forming drugs". Using `.114` as a narcotics proxy imports ~260 rum-running
documents. Excluding `811.114` leaves 180 documents in the family, of which `893.114` (China, 46) is
the real narcotics file.

## 3. Two findings I did not expect, both checkable

### (a) FRUS goes near-silent on narcotics from 1948 to 1960

There is **no narcotics chapter anywhere between the 1947 Mexico chapter and the 1961–63
"Narcotics" chapter** — a thirteen-year hole in a topic FRUS had carried in nearly every annual
volume since 1906. It is not that State stopped: 98 documents dated 1948–1960 across 41 volumes
mention a narcotics term, scattered inside chapters about Turkish aid, China, Iran, Mexico and the
UN. But the rate decays inside the gap — 1948:12, 1950:14, 1951:15, 1953:10, then 1958:1, 1959:2,
1960:1.

This is a statement about **FRUS's editorial practice**, not about the Department. It is the same
period in which, per my Task 1 profile, the series was turning from compilation into selection.
Whether State's narcotics work also fell off, or merely fell out of the compilation, is exactly the
question the archives in §4 would answer — and the memo cannot.

### (b) The one chapter that names narcotics in the 1950s contains none

`frus1952-54v08/ch7` is headed *"UNITED STATES RELATIONS WITH TURKEY: … AND THE PROBLEM OF ILLICIT
TRAFFIC IN NARCOTICS"*. It holds 32 documents.

```sql
-- of the chapter's 32 documents, how many contain any narcotics term?   0
-- in the whole volume:
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM frus_documents
WHERE frus_documents MATCH 'opium OR narcot OR poppi OR drug OR heroin OR morphin'
  AND volume_id='frus1952-54v08';                                        -- 3
```
Three in the entire volume, and **none of the three is in that chapter** (they are `d192`, `d540`,
`d577` — the UK, an Office memorandum, and a 1953 Moscow despatch). The heading is the only trace of
Turkish opium in the volume. Its 32 documents are Ambassador McGhee on aid, oil and NSC papers, and
they carry classes `611.82` and `782.5-MSP`, not `.114`.

So the single most promising-looking 1950s heading in the corpus is, on the printed text, empty on
your question. I flag it because a keyword-free scope built from headings alone would have made it a
centrepiece. If Turkish opium in 1952–54 matters to you, the documents are not in FRUS and §4 says
where to look instead.

**Do not gloss the 1950s and 1960s classes with this table.** The classification was renumbered in

The gloss column above stops at 1949 for that reason, not for lack of trying.

### CHANNEL B — POINTED-AT (`external_citations`, what the footnotes cite)
Over the same 527 documents: **23 citing documents, 32 footnote references.** Repository:
Department of State, 32 of 32. One lot file (`57 F 72`). Classes cited include `711.56382`,
`882.2553`, `611.82`, `341.9`, `782.11`, `500.C1197`.

**This channel is effectively unavailable for your period, and that is structural.** Lot-file and
presidential-library footnote citations are a post-1945 editorial practice; the 1906–1947 volumes
that hold 463 of your 527 documents are barely footnoted at all. 23 of 527 is not a thin result to
work around — it is the measurement. Do not read Channel B's silence as the editors having nothing
to point at.

The two channels are **two different numbers over one scope** and must never be added.

**The 1906–1910 file is online in full.** `central-files-index.json` puts Numerical File case 774

**4,210 images.** The FRUS chapters for 1908–1911 cite 774/383B, 774/393, 774/438–439, 774/691,
774/693, 774/699 — i.e. rolls 106 and 108. The printed chapters are a selection from a file you can
read end to end without leaving your desk. That is the strongest single lead in this memo.

**Everything after 1910 requires College Park.** `digitized-ranges-index.json` covers just 18
decimal classes — visa and passport files (131, 133) and the First World War German-relations file
(763.72). Checked individually: `511.4`, `893.114`, `500.C1197`, `890.114`, `891.114`, `812.114`,

League file are paper.

**The offline series stack cannot help before 1940, and this is measured, not assumed.**
`series-facts-index.json` holds 695 series; **11 start before 1940 and exactly 1 before 1910**
(a Panama City consulate series, 1909–1935). Series NAID 654171 — the Numerical File itself — is
**not in it**. So for 1906–1939 you get the roll and the class from the bundled indexes and nothing
about creator, extent, restrictions or facility. That is a limit of the offline stack, not an
archival absence, and I would not report it as one.

**The index's own person tables are useless here, and my Task 2 profile predicts exactly why.**

above, and treat every count as a floor.

1961 treaty. It is a false friend in the early volumes. Its real distribution is 1960s: 18,
1970s: 34.

Three things fall out of this that the SQLite index could not have told you:

1. **`opium` is the word — 5,747 occurrences, 4.5× the next term.** Any scope not built on it is
   built on the wrong century. `narcotics` and `narcotic drugs` are the *later* vocabulary.
2. **`habit-forming drugs`, the official name of the 1924 Geneva conference, occurs 43 times.**
   The index cannot count it at all (porter folds it into `habit`, 1,724 documents). The conference
   is famous; its own phrase is rare. Do not scope on it.
3. **The counting surfaces disagree by about 10% on the control phrase** — 11,595 stripped against
   10,505 raw, because TEI markup splits the phrase mid-string. On the narcotics terms the delta is
   1–2 occurrences. Always say which surface a density came from; I have.

**I have not separated document text from apparatus.** These are whole-file counts including front
matter, footnotes and the index. `body_text` in SQLite likewise blends document and editor. Any
density claim from either surface would be a claim about FRUS's language, not about diplomats'.

## 7. What I would search for next

1. **Read M862 rolls 104–108 first.** 4,210 digitised images, the whole opium file behind
   the 1908–1911 chapters, free and online. This is the cheapest large gain available.
2. **Work the `.114` volumes with no narcotics chapter** — the 24 listed in §2, beginning with
   `frus1933v03` (six documents at `893.114-N`). The filing channel found them; the topical one
   could not.
3. **Test the 1948–1960 silence at College Park.** The classes to pull are `511.4A*` (conferences)
   and the `*.114` country files for the years FRUS skipped. None is digitised. If State's files are
   thick where FRUS is thin, the gap is editorial; if thin, it is institutional. The corpus cannot
   distinguish these and should not be asked to.
4. **For Turkey 1951–54, do not start from FRUS.** The chapter heading is the only narcotics content
   in the volume. Start from the Central Files for those years and the Bureau of Narcotics' own
   records — which are Treasury, not RG 59, and therefore outside every index I used here.
5. **On people, use FTS strings, not the person tables**, and treat Stuart J. Fuller (21 documents,
   11 volumes) as the highest-yield State-side name in the interwar material.

## 8. Limits of this memo

- Every count is conditional on this library. It holds all 552 published volumes, so the limit is
  FRUS's publication frontier, not a partial download — but FRUS is a **selection**, and none of
  §2's numbers is a measure of what State did.
- `body_text` includes editorial footnotes, so a term hit may be the editor's word, not the
  document's. I have not split them and this database cannot.
- I glossed decimal classes only for 1910–1949, the one schedule that ships. The 1950s and 1960s
  classes — including `341.9`, which carries the entire Kennedy-era chapter — are unresolved here
  by design, and `611.82` shows what resolving them anyway would have produced.
- Channel A and Channel B are separate counts over one scope; §4 never adds them.
- Subject tags played no part in this scope. Where I used the bundled `collection-usage-index.json`
  I verified its encoding before trusting it: my first reading of its `n`/`v` arrays was backwards
  and put decimal-file citations in 1860s volumes. The artifact's own `coverage.notesWithAClassKey`
  = 190,407 equals the sum of `n`, which is what settled it. The corrected figures are the ones
  above.
