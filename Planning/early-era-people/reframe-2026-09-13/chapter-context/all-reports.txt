HEADLINE: The app's pre-1906 classifier now runs offline, compiled from the repo's own Swift, and passes 55 of 55 checks copied from the unit tests. In 1861–1899, Source Explorer places 70.3% of documents (22,846 of 32,478) in a diplomatic series with a country. It places 4.5% as notes from a foreign legation and only 1.7% (567) as consular despatches with a post; 23.1% get nothing. The country comes from the first section title, reading from the volume root down, whose first normalised key has a NARA roll covering the date. It is the chapter's country, not the post the document was written from. It fails on Roman-numeral titles, so frus1873p1v1 is country-arranged yet resolves 0 of 310 documents.

[R1-country-selection] (documented) Source Explorer does not pick 'the country chapter'. It walks the section path from the volume root down to the leaf, classifies the document once per title, and stops at the first title that produces any classification with at least one roll. That makes the NARA roll vocabulary (67 geo keys) the only test of whether a title is a country. Only the first normalised key is looked up, so 'Sweden and Norway' checks 'sweden' alone, and a long title ending '...United States and Great Britain.' checks its opening words and never resolves.

[R2-series-rules] (documented) classify(header:dateline:chapterCountry:) applies nine checks in a fixed order, and the first that matches returns. Matching is on the lowercased dateline (and header where noted). (1) The header or dateline contains 'special agent', 'special commissioner' or 'special mission': if the dateline has 'department of state' the result is specialAgentsInstructions, otherwise specialAgentsDespatches; medium, no geography. (2) The dateline names another executive office (war, navy, treasury, post office, justice, interior, agriculture or commerce department, 'executive mansion', 'white house'): lettersReceived, high, no geography. (3) A foreign consulate: the dateline contains 'consul' and no US marker ('united states', 'american', 'u.s.', 'u. s.'), and matches either `consulate(-general)? of \w` or a word other than 'the' before 'consulate'; notesFromForeignConsuls, high, no geography. (4) The dateline contains 'consulate' or 'consular': consularDespatches, high, with the post city from consularPostKey as the geography. If no city can be isolated it returns [] outright, and the chapter is never consulted. (5) geoKeys is set to GeoKeyNormalizer.keys(chapterCountry). A dateline naming a US mission ('legation of the united states', 'embassy of the united states', 'american legation', 'american embassy', 'united states legation', 'u. s. legation', 'u.s. legation') gives despatches, high, if geoKeys is non-empty, otherwise []. (6) 'department of state' in the dateline: with a chapter, instructions and notesTo, both medium. If the header also contains 'consul', add consularInstructions and notesToForeignConsuls (medium, no geography). If a cabinet office follows the header's first ' to ' (secretary of war, navy, treasury, interior, agriculture or commerce; attorney general; postmaster general), add domesticLetters (medium). Return the list, which may be empty. (7) With no geoKeys, return []. (8) 'washington' plus 'legation' or 'embassy' in the dateline: notesFrom, high. (9) Fallback: a non-blank dateline with neither 'washington' nor 'department of state' gives despatches, medium ('Datelined abroad'). Anything else returns []. A 'Washington, <date>' dateline with no office named therefore gets nothing.

[R3-consular-post] (measured) consularPostKey drops everything from the first 'Month D, YYYY' onward and splits the rest on commas. It returns the first segment containing none of consul, united states, american, legation or embassy, canonicalised. Failing that, it takes the text after '\bat\s+' inside a consul segment. Otherwise nil. The post key is a bare lowercased city ('havana'); the consular series carries 495 distinct roll geo keys.

[R4-normalizer] (measured) GeoKeyNormalizer.keys trims, strips a leading 'Vol(ume|.) N:', cuts at the first ':', and splits on ' & ', ', ' and ' and '. Each part is canonicalised: trim whitespace and '.,;', lowercase, collapse spaces, apply the alias table. It has no rule for Roman-numeral prefixes ('III.—Argentine Republic.', 'XXIX.—Spain.'), '(Continued.)', legation-chapter titles ('British legation.', 'Correspondence with the Mexican legation.'), 'Chili' (the aliases cover 'chilian' and 'chilean' only) or 'Rome.'. Those chapters fall through to nothing.

[R5-era-gate] (measured) The 1906 gate is applied per document, on the year of its own dateline, not the volume's. documentYear = DocumentView.extractYear(entry.dateline): the year from datelineDateISO (the first 'Month D, YYYY'), otherwise the first \b(1[89]dd|20[0-2]d)\b. A document with no dateline, or no year in it, is never classified. Header and dateline are the raw document_cache columns.

[R6-confidence-and-enclosures] (documented) There are only two confidence levels: high ('Likely') and medium ('Possible'). Enclosures are classified from their own opener with chapterCountry nil. An enclosure therefore places only through a consular post or a series matched by date (foreign-consul notes, letters received, special agents, the consul pair, domestic letters); a US-legation or bare-city enclosure gets nothing. The doc comment at SourceExplorerView.swift 1128-1129 says an enclosure 'inherits the chapter country', but the code passes nil (CentralFilesClassifier.swift 117-125, 137-139) and never uses the `path` parameter. The harness does NOT run the enclosure pass, which needs a TEI AST parse.

UNKNOWNS:
 - Accuracy is unmeasured. No keyed archival or identity gold exists, so every figure here describes what the app DOES, not whether the series or country is right. The chapter-versus-post mismatch (C5) is a proxy.
 - The enclosure pass (SourceExplorerView 1118-1182) is not reproduced; it needs FRUSDocumentParser plus IndexingPipeline.extractEnclosureOpeners over TEI. The code passes chapterCountry nil, so enclosures add no chapter country.
 - The live index (version 50) predates PR #1291. Structures and document_cache rows for frus1873p1v2 and the split-set parts could change after a re-index; not checked.
 - The Department-outbound pair (7,925 + 1,674 documents) is by design ambiguous between the US minister in the chapter country and that country's minister in Washington. Subchapter titles such as 'Correspondence with the Russian legation.' would settle it, but the classifier never reads them.
 - The despatch_serial column and despatchSerial fetch were not examined.

HEADLINE: Yes, but mostly as a check, not as new answers. I turned each document's classifier output (the app's real Swift classifier, via the harness) into role hypotheses for its marked from/to names and matched them against the people POCOM lists as holding those posts on the document's date. POCOM's surname-by-year rule already names exactly one person for 53.7% of the 84,234 from/to rows in the 61 no-list volumes of 1861–1899. The chapter rule names one person for 51.6% using only what Source Explorer shows (arm A), or 61.6% using the classifier's role and direction without the NARA-roll test plus a repaired title crosswalk (arm B). Most of that rise is not evidence. 8,733 of arm B's 10,973 rows that move from several people to one are 'Mr. Seward' in the 1860s, assigned to the Secretary over the Assistant Secretary by rank alone. In 117 Seward-headed documents F. W. Seward signed. Setting those rows aside, arm B's combined answer names one person for 53.5% of rows, the same as POCOM's rule. The real value is a post for POCOM's single names: 90.4% of them in arm B, and 47.5% name the US chief in the chapter's country. The rule also flags 2,415 rows (2.9%) where POCOM's person does not hold any hypothesised post, and picks the right namesake in 2,240 other rows ('Osborn' Chile vs Argentina). In 1900–1905 (13,160 rows) it adds almost nothing new: 10 rows move from several to one. It adds a post for 91.7% of POCOM's single names and flags 509 contradictions. Everything here is what the rule decides, not whether it is right.

[M0-method] (measured) Scope: the 70 no-list volumes up to 1905 (61 in 1861–1899, 9 in 1900–1905), taken from ~/frus-ner-raw/scope.json. The population is every marked row with t=from/to: 84,234 and 13,160 rows. Band is taken from the volume name, as in measure_pocom.py.

The three answers:
- (i) POCOM surname-by-year. measure_pocom.py is imported, not copied: surname_of, text_of, load_pocom, and tei_doc_years for the TEI year with ±1. The 1861–1899 classes reproduce pocom.json's by_band marked_fromto exactly: 84,234 rows; 45,258 one; 11,995 several; 8,595 nobody; 18,386 unknown.
- (ii) Chapter rule. Take POCOM officeholders holding a hypothesised post on the document's exact date: the dateline date from the harness, otherwise the index date. Keep those whose POCOM surname equals surname_of(name). Department posts have two tiers:
  - tier 1: secretary and secretary-ad-interim;
  - tier 2: assistant-secretary, second-assistant-secretary, third-assistant-secretary and chief-clerk, used only if tier 1 and the chief posts match nobody.
  A chief post runs from the appointed, arrived or started date to the ended date. An appointment with no start and no end is excluded (221, e.g. declined nominations). A served appointment with no end closes at the next chief's start (84); 155 stay open.
- (iii) Combined: (ii) when it names exactly one person, otherwise (i)'s one. The strict variant also drops (i)'s one when it is contradicted.

Roles are assigned only for the document's own head: the header string, located in the R-0 text. Of the 1861–1899 rows, 61,068 are head rows, 23,128 are enclosure rows and 38 heads could not be located. Enclosure rows get an undirected hypothesis: the Department, or the chapter country's chief.

Arm A uses only what Source Explorer shows (a classification that has a roll). Arm B uses the Swift classifier's categories for the first section title (root to leaf) that the crosswalk maps to a POCOM territory, without the roll test, and also reads legation subchapter titles. No classifier rule was ported. Categories, confidences, geo keys and title keys all come from the Swift harness. The role table, crosswalk and head locator are new Python.

UNKNOWNS:
 - Accuracy is unmeasured. No identity gold is keyed (m1a-eval-candidates.csv, 0 of 300), so corroboration, contradiction and several→one are decisions, not correct answers. The validation lens must score them. The best scoring targets are probably the two contradiction subclasses (R5) and the 2,240 non-convention several→one rows.
 - The rule matches surnames only. Checking initials against POCOM forenames would reclassify at least 164 Seward-type rows and probably many enclosure names; not built.
 - Temporary acting secretaryships are not in POCOM, and the chief-clerk file ends in 1855. 'Mr. Hunter' in 1865–66 and similar acting officers show as contradictions for a data reason. How many of the 859 'no post' contradictions are POCOM gaps rather than legation secretaries is unmeasured.
 - Chief intervals use appointed, arrived or started through ended. Despatches signed before arrival or after recall fall in the 'within 180 days' bucket (373 rows). No slack is applied to the decisions themselves.
 - Arm B's hypotheses come from the classifier run on the first mapped title, without the roll test. Source Explorer does not do this, so arm B shows what the strategy could do, not what the app does. The role table and crosswalk are new Python, not ports of app code.
 - Enclosure rows get undirected hypotheses (the Department or the chapter chief). Their 32.9% chapter-one rate (7,308 of 22,204) is not comparable to head rows (44,572 of 58,311).
 - Office-only heads are sized from phrases, not parsed slots. The 'unmarked' counts include heads where the name beside the office is marked. 'The President' and 'minister of foreign affairs' have no local source.
 - Inherited from the harness: the live index predates PR #1291, and the harness does not reproduce the enclosure pass.

[D1-rule-as-implemented] (documented) No coverage-lens code exists in the scratchpad, so I implemented the rule as written in the brief and state it exactly here.
- S (surname-by-year): measure_pocom.load_pocom, surname_of and text_of, imported verbatim. The year is the TEI doc-dateTime-min year. It picks when exactly one POCOM officeholder with that exact-case surname has any appointment span covering year ±1. Classes: unknown, nobody, one, several.
- CH (chapter rule alone): K is the first section-path title whose first normalised key is a diplomatic roll geo key, per the real Swift. The direction comes from the real classify output at that title: diplomaticDespatches gives from = chief of mission in K, to = Department; diplomaticInstructions (with notesTo) gives from = Department, to = mission K; notesFromForeignMissions and consularDespatches give to = Department. The mission set is the POCOM missions-countries chiefs whose territory or contemporary-territory id normalises, via the real Swift, to K (51 territories reach vocabulary keys). A chief is in office from appointed/started to ended; if ended is missing, to the next start in the same file; primary tolerance is 0 days before and 60 after. Non-ad-interim holders are preferred, and a single person is picked. The Department side picks the unique POCOM `secretary` in office. CH does not read the name.
- COMB: every in-office chief for K (all roles), or every positions-principals holder on the Department side, whose POCOM surname equals the mention's last 1–3 tokens (letters only, casefolded); a single person is picked.
- Chapter variants: APP (raw titles), STRIP (numeral removed), SHOWN (Source Explorer's own shown classifications with rolls, year < 1906). The date is datelineDateISO, else the index date_iso, else the TEI date.
No app rule was ported to Python: classification, section path, title normalisation and territory normalisation all run in Swift. The direction mapping, office windows and gold matching are new code, not ports.

[L2-reuse-without-second-implementation] (documented) The repo's rule is that generators write through the declarations the app reads through. Reusing the classifier therefore means lifting its pure core into such a kit, not porting it. Four things tie it to the app today, all found by the harness, which needed each of them. [P1-carriers-that-must-not-be-used] (documented) None of the three existing identity carriers can hold a POCOM-slug candidate. (1) PersonClusterInput.authorityId. Records sharing an id are unioned across blocks before the pairwise pass. decide() merges when both carry the same id. The seal 'Reconciled identity' lights on effectiveAuthorityId != nil, and the POCOM career shows through authorityEntry.s. Setting it from a candidate would force-merge, seal and show a career on a machine guess: the §5(5) defect. (2) mustLink.
