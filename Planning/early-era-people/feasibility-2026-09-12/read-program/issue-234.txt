### But it does not address what this issue describes

The body says the people browser "is limited to volumes where editors created lists of people in the volume. This excludes the first ~70 years of documentation." Measured against the manifest and the owner's index:

| volumes | 552 |
|---|---|

**POCOM cannot fix it.** POCOM records carry no FRUS anchor of any kind — a person file is `{id, persName, birth, death, career-type, residence}`, and assignments live in `missions-*/` keyed by POCOM slug, not by volume. ## The deferred M1/M2/M3 program is now a live doc — and two of its premises were wrong

The program was defined inside `Planning/Completed/Issues-233-243-Plan.md`, archived in the 2026-08-05 cleanup as a "closed issue-wave plan". The wave closed; this didn't. It is now **`Planning/People-Early-Era-Program.md`** (PR #739), with the archived row corrected in place so the old claim cannot be re-read as current.

### The `persons-new.xml` reframe does not hold

The plan's 2026-07-09 revision says that drop turns M1 from "NER from scratch" into "adopt + review the colleague's extraction — much smaller program". Measured, it reaches **2 of the 268 volumes that have no person list**, and 2 of the 129 pre-1930 ones, out of the 111 volumes it covers.

That is structural: its anchors are `volume/persons#ref`, so they can only land in volumes that *already have* a persons page. It enriches the covered corpus and cannot extend into the uncovered one. **M1 is not smaller than originally scoped.**

### The gap is bigger than the volume count suggests

268 of 552 volumes have no list — but they hold **199,246 documents, 62.9% of the corpus**, because they are the large early annual volumes. 82,862 of those are pre-1930.

### But the problem is better-posed than "NER"

Those volumes are not unmarked text. Across all 268 they carry **253,919 editor-marked `<persName>` elements** — 141,064 typed `from`, 95,837 `to`, 17,018 untyped; 13,914 distinct name strings, 3,253 appearing five or more times — with **zero** links to any identity.

A *covered* volume links its mentions with `corresp="#p_W1"` back to an editor list entry (`<persName xml:id="p_W1">Wilhelmina</persName> Queen of the Netherlands.`); `frus1915Supp` carries `corresp` on 4,033 of its 5,392 names. The difference between covered and uncovered is **not whether the names are marked up — it is whether there is a list for them to point at.**

So the core work is **reconciliation, not detection**. And with 93.3% of the names sitting in `from`/`to` correspondence headers, POCOM is load-bearing here in a way it was not for the M0 work that shipped in #736: sender + recipient + document date + who held which post when is a three-way constraint.

One thing deliberately **not** claimed: 253,919 is the count of names the editors chose to mark up. What share of *all* person mentions that represents is unmeasured, and detection may still be needed for people named only in running prose. Measuring it is the first task in the new **M1a** gate, ahead of the ground-truth eval set and a measurement of POCOM's constraint strength — because the program's size depends on all three answers.

The eval-first, pilot-first, offline-batch discipline is carried forward unchanged.
