# C-3 steering tier (R16) — what the vectors should add on this question, written before the first cell

Written 2026-10-30, before calibration, the canary or any cell, so that the result can contradict it
(`Planning/C-3-Steering-Tier-Design.md` §1). The figures are the choosing session's, from the C-3
index copy (version 71) and the bundle's Tier-1 binary at digest `89481b18…`. That session's
scripts and outputs were deleted before the first cell. The question is the owner's, chosen
2026-10-30 from five candidates.

**The scope a session will find.** About 2,900 documents in some 440 volumes use *corruption*,
*bribery*, *graft*, *venality*, *kickbacks* or *embezzlement*, in every era. About one in 75 sits
under a chapter heading that names corruption, bribery or a scandal (37 of 2,866). The rest sit
under country chapters: Korea, the Philippines, China, Iran after 1953, the equipment-recovery
mission in Vietnam, Cuba, Haiti. So a session scopes by full text, not by headings, and the heading
scope that swallowed Cuba's neighbours is small here.

**The vocabulary moves.** *Peculation* peaks in the 1860s and is gone by the 1930s; *malversation*
runs from the 1900s to the 1930s; *embezzlement* peaks in the 1900s; *fraud* in the 1870s;
*kickback* and *nepotism* appear only after the 1930s. *Corruption* itself runs through the series.

**What the vectors should add.**
- **Reach.** Of the 25 nearest neighbours of three high-ranking documents per era, about three in
  four sat outside both the heading scope and the full-text scope. Some of these are about corrupt
  practice in other words: a 1943 airgram on the Cuban congressional inquiry into "the Godoy sugar
  insurance deal", and a 1971 Haiti telegram on "the avarice of his less honest collaborators".
  Most are same-country political documents that do not bear on the question. Expected: one to
  four strict vector-reached documents in each B run, and more in B than in A on the same cell,
  because B is told the flagged join is required and A is given five operations as equals.
- **Across eras.** Cross-era reach is rarer: neighbours mostly stay within a decade of their
  anchor (127 of 343 out-of-scope neighbours were in another decade). Expected: at most one
  cross-era vector-reached document a run.
- **Near-duplicates.** Expected in the nineteenth-century annuals and the 1894 appendices, where
  correspondence is printed twice. B should cite one copy of each and count it once.
- **Vocabulary.** Every vectors-tier collection carried vector words into its notes. Expected: V8
  violated in most A runs, obeyed in all four B runs.
- **Run 9** writes "VECTORS: none" and opens no vector file.

**What would contradict this.** No strict vector-reached document in any B run. Or A at or above
B on reach in both cells. Or B losing more of the 35 items than A.
