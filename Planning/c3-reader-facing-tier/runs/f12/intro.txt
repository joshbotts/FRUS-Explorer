What FRUS answers directly: about two dozen printed documents, from 1950 to 1977, show how officials of the State Department and the U.S. Information Agency judged America's appeal in East Asia and the Pacific. They wanted to project an image of American strength, reliability and friendship, aimed mostly at elites, through local voices and with low American visibility; they measured success by press placement, attendance, demand for English, the warmth of a visit and, from about 1970, opinion surveys; and they believed Asian audiences judged the United States less by its domestic life than by its bilateral conduct and its staying power. After 1975 their own assessments say credibility had become the message.

Where FRUS points for the rest: the printed documents came mainly from the U.S. Information Agency's records (Record Group 306: the Director's Subject Files, 1968–1972, Entry A1–42; the Director's subject files of 1963–1969, Entries UD WW 257 and UD WW 193; the Office of Policy and Plans, Entry P–207; the Executive Secretariat's correspondence files, 1973–1980, Entry P–104), from State Department central files (Record Group 59: decimal files 511.90, 511.51G, 790.5, 792.5 and 611.98, and the 1977 Central Foreign Policy File), from the Operations Coordinating Board files (Lot 61 D 385) and from the Kennedy, Johnson and Nixon presidential records. The editors also cite, without printing, the Office of Southeast Asian Affairs' subject files for 1955–1956 (Lot 58 D 726, National Archives identifier 2544398, access possibly restricted). The app's bundled indexes give no National Archives identifier or access status for the USIA entries.

What this request cannot get from FRUS: the phrase "soft power" does not occur in any of the 21 volumes scanned (the officials wrote of "image", "psychological" objectives and "public diplomacy"); the underlying opinion surveys are summarised, not printed; the audiences' own voices are absent because FRUS prints U.S. records; and no East Asia or public diplomacy volume after 1980 is in this corpus. The surveys sit in USIA's research files in Record Group 306.
