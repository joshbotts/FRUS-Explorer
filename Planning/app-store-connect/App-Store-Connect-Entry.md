## Four decisions, and two offers

Each is yours. The first three were not in the plan of record's §2 until this sheet was written:
it listed "the age rating" without its answers, and said nothing of the other two.

1. **The age rating.** Apple replaced its questionnaire in 2025, and its definition of *Mature or
   Suggestive Themes* now names "war or political strife". No honest set of answers gives this
   app 4+. The answers in [step 1.5](#15-age-rating) give **13+**; answering *Frequent* for that
   one item gives 16+. Recommended: 13+.
2. **Trader status under the EU's Digital Services Act.** Apple asks every developer to declare
   whether they are a trader, "in order to distribute apps in the EU". A trader's address, phone
   number and email address are shown on the product page in the 27 EU countries; a non-trader
   gives nothing. It is a legal status, and Apple's page leaves the assessment to you.
   [Step 0](#0-before-the-app-your-account) has Apple's test in its words.
3. **China mainland.** Decision 31 is every storefront. Apple's reference says Chinese law
   requires a filing number for "some apps" on that storefront, and a publishing permit for
   "apps with books and magazine content". Nothing in the repository records either for this app.
   Recommended: if the availability page asks for either, leave China mainland out and keep
   every other storefront
   ([step 3](#3-pricing-and-availability)).
4. **How 1.0 is released.** Recommended: a manual release, on both platforms. The website has a
   coming-soon version and a launch version, and a manual release lets you switch the site,
   release both apps and announce on one day of your choosing.

The two offers are in the listing draft, each counted, and neither is in the paste files until
you say so:

- **Keywords:** `relations` in place of `FRUS`, since Apple's reference says not to repeat a word
  of the app's name (the draft's §1).
- **Description:** your sentence on Editors' Annotations from the home page, at the end of the
  BUILT ON THE EDITORS' WORK paragraph, which brings the field to 3,584 of 4,000 (the draft's §2).

## 0. Before the app: your account

**Business ▸ Agreements.** Under Compliance, beside Digital Services Act, see whether a status is
already declared. If it is not, **Complete Compliance Requirements** asks for it. Apple's page:

- "Even if you don't distribute apps in the EU, you'll still need to declare a trader status."
- The factors it gives for being a trader include whether "your app includes In-App Purchases, or
  if it's a paid or ad-sponsored app" and whether you "develop your app in connection with your
  trade, business, craft, or profession". It says a developer is "unlikely to be a trader" who is
  a hobbyist and made an app "with no intention of commercializing it".
- A non-trader chooses "This is not a trader account" and "won't need to provide any contact
  information". A trader who is an individual gives an address or a P.O. Box, a phone number and
  an email address, which Apple publishes on the product page in the EU.

What bears on it, from the repository: the app is free in every storefront, with no purchase, no
advertising and no account, and its description calls it an independent, private-capacity tool.
The declaration is yours to make. The same status is then set for the app, under **App
Information ▸ App Store Regulations and Permits ▸ Digital Services Act**.

## 1. App Information

One page for the iPhone, iPad and Mac apps together.

### 1.1 Name and subtitle

[paste/name.txt](paste/name.txt) and [paste/subtitle.txt](paste/subtitle.txt). Primary language:
English (U.S.). The app ships no localization.

### 1.2 Category

**Primary: Reference.** Both apps' `Info.plist` declare `public.app-category.reference`, and
Apple's reference says the primary category "should match the category you set in Xcode" for a
Mac app. **Secondary: Education**, or none. Education is offered because teachers are one of the
launch kit's three audiences; nothing in the repository decides it.

### 1.3 Content rights

The page asks whether the app contains, shows or accesses third-party content. It does: the FRUS
volumes, which the Office of the Historian publishes and which are in the public domain, and the
National Archives Catalog, which a reader may query with their own key. So the answer is that it
does and that you have the rights to it. Apple's reference gives the rule and not the options:
the app "must have all the necessary rights to that content or be otherwise permitted to use it
under the laws of each App Store country or region". The options' wording is from memory.

### 1.4 License agreement

Apple's standard agreement. No custom one is entered (decision 17).

### 1.5 Age rating

**App Information ▸ Age Ratings ▸ Set Up Age Ratings.** One rating covers every platform. The
dialog first lists in-app controls and capabilities, and then asks for "the appropriate level of
frequency for each content description". The definitions quoted here are Apple's.

**In-app controls and capabilities: select none.**

| Item | Answer | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| Parental Controls, Age Assurance | no | It has neither. |
| Unrestricted Web Access | no | Apple: "Users can navigate to any webpage within the app or freely browse the web." The app has a built-in browser for the links in About and the FRUS Research Guide and for the quick start. It has Back, Forward and Open in Browser, and no field for an address or a search. It does not stop a page's own links from leading on to other sites. If App Review reads that as unrestricted, the answer is yes and the rating is 16+. |
| User-Generated Content, Social Media, Messaging and Chat | no | Notes, highlights and collections go to the reader's own devices and files, and to no other user. |
| Advertising | no | None. |

**Content descriptions.**

| Item | Answer | Why |

War and political strife are the series' subject, in official prose. *Infrequent* is the answer news apps gave to the earlier form of this question, as far as I recall, and that is not from Apple's page. *Frequent* is the stricter reading, and makes the rating 16+. |
| Sexual Content or Nudity; Graphic Sexual Content and Nudity | None | |

**The result is 13+**, by Apple's table: infrequent realistic violence, infrequent references to
alcohol and frequent guns or other weapons each sit at 13+, and nothing above it. With *Frequent*
for Mature or Suggestive Themes it is 16+.

What the choice costs: a device whose content restrictions are set below the rating does not show
or run the app. At 13+ that leaves out a device restricted for a child under 13. At 16+ it also
leaves out one restricted for a 13- to 15-year-old, which touches classroom use and none of the
launch kit's three audiences otherwise. Under **Age Categories and Override** choose
**Not Applicable**: the app is not Made for Kids, and an override can only raise the rating. Leave
the Age Suitability URL empty.

These answers are my reading of Apple's definitions against what the documents hold. They are
your declaration, and App Review can ask for a different one.

## 6. Submit

On each version page: check the Build section, then **Add for Review**, then **Submit for
Review**. Apple's page says a version is added to a new submission or to an existing draft one,
and that "All items submitted together must be Accepted to complete the submission." So one
submission for each platform keeps a question about one app from holding the other, and with
manual release on both they still go out together. Whether App Store Connect offers the two
platforms one shared submission could not be seen from here.

Then, by the plan of record: tag the submitted commit `1.0-release` (decision 113).

## Not required now

**Accessibility Nutrition Labels.** Apple: "providing these labels will be voluntary to start",
and later "you'll be required to share accessibility support details to submit new apps and app
updates". A label claims that a person can complete all of the app's common tasks with that
feature: VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text, Dark Interface, Differentiate Without Color Alone,
Sufficient Contrast or Reduced Motion, and two for video and audio. The repository tests parts of
this (the reader's contrast, VoiceOver on Search's rows, several bars at the larger text sizes),
and nothing walks every common task with one feature, which is Apple's test. So no answers are
drafted, and the section can be left unanswered at 1.0.

**iPhone Duo screenshots.** Apple's page: "Starting in April 2027, screenshots will be required
for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later." This Mac's Xcode is 27.2, whose iOS SDK is 27.2,
so the rule does not reach 1.0 now and will reach an update submitted from April 2027.
