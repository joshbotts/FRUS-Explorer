**The two questions, answered.**

1. **The reader-facing revisions hold under the block, with one weak rule.** The READER-FACING
   OUTPUT section (guide §12 since v1.29) got the behaviour in 31 of the 36 reader items scored
   across twelve runs: O3, the three-part bottom line, in 12 of 12; O1, no SQL, column, id pair

   controls) in the two `medium` cells S-med and F-med. The block as a whole scored 396 of 408
   items obeyed across the twelve runs (0.971), above C-0c's 0.952.
2. **Which model and effort to recommend**, for this question, on these measurements: **Opus 5.5 at

## 3. The three readings (design §5, pre-registered)

- **Fastest**: the lowest run wall clock among cells at or above C-0c's floor of 0.952 with zero
  fabrications is **O-med, 18.2 and 19.4 minutes**. The fastest cell of all, S-med at 13.5
  minutes, sits below the floor (0.912, and 4 of 5 reader items) and is a warning, not a
  recommendation. F-med (24.4 min) and F-max (34.4 min) are below the floor too; O-high (27.1 min,
  0.941) misses it by one item.
- **Best value**: the highest quality per weekly point, instruments included, is **O-med at 0.667**
  (1.000 over 1.5 points), then S-high 0.500, O-high 0.471, O-max 0.394, S-med 0.365, F-high
  0.324, F-max 0.304, F-med 0.251, S-max 0.250.
- **Highest quality**: three cells tie at 1.000 with no refuter error — S-high, S-max and O-med —
  and the tie goes to the judge's final ranking, where **S-high's second run is first** and its
  first run fourth, S-max fifth, O-med ninth and tenth. S-high is also the cheaper of the two
  Sonnet cells by half and three times faster than S-max.

**Against the expected shape** (design §5). *Effort matters more than model on compliance*: not
on this question. Every `medium` cell was imperfect (S-med 0.912, F-med 0.941) except Opus's two
perfect runs, and `max` did not buy compliance (F-max 0.912, O-max 1.000 then 0.971). The column
mattered more: Opus and Sonnet reached perfect scores at every effort but `medium` for Sonnet,
and the Fable column never did (0.912, 0.971, 0.941). *Model matters more than effort on the
reader items*: the reader items were nearly saturated (10 of 12 runs at 5 of 5); the two misses
were both `medium` cells, which is an effort effect, not a model one. *The Fable column costs more
than twice the Opus column's for less than a one-item gain*: it cost 3 points a cell against Opus's
1 to 3 and gained nothing; it was the one column below the floor at two of three efforts. The
diagonal shows an ordering (S-med 0.729 < O-high 0.941 < F-max 0.912 is not monotone), so the
question is not too easy; the result is that the two cheaper columns answer it as well or better.

## 4. Limitations, as recorded before the result and as found

1. **n = 1 per cell, n = 2 for three.** The three replicated cells repeated their first result on
   compliance (S-high and O-med perfect twice; O-max perfect then one item down), on the
   refuter's zero errors, and on the reader's five of five. The wall clock repeated within two
   minutes for O-med and S-high and within eighteen for O-max. The meter's whole-point rounding
   showed: O-med read +1 then +2, O-max +2 then +3, at near-identical token counts.
2. **One question, and an open one.** The scopes differed by cell, as the refuters' first rows
   record (`verdicts.json`, `refute:*.scope`): from 25 volumes (S-med) and 38 (S-high) to 137
   (O-max) and 157 (S-max), by chapter headings, by proximity search, or both. The readings
   compare answers of different scopes; the design knew it would.
3. **The instruments share a model with one column.** The Fable column scored lowest on
   compliance under a Fable scorer and was ranked high by a Fable judge (F-max first six times,
   sixth at the end). Neither looks like favour. The judge's items are yes/no on checkable
   properties and saturated; the ranking is the tie-breaker only, and it decided the highest-quality
   reading between three non-Fable cells.
4. **The instruments cost more than some cells.** About one point of the weekly meter per cell,
   measured on the Cuba kit before any cell, and about three points of the Fable window per cell;
   over twelve runs the instruments were 36 of the Fable window's 52 points. The value reading
   divides by the gross figure, as §8 item 3a says.
5. **Weekly usage is a plan's meter.** The table carries the run's output tokens and wall clock
   beside it, and names the plan (Max). The whole run was 14,566,758 output tokens (thinking
   included), 56.5 M cache-write and 1.33 B cache-read tokens, and 2,848 tool calls across 53
   agents in 13 workflow runs; 15 h 02 m of agent time summed over the agents (a cell's three
   instruments ran in parallel), 11 h 06 m elapsed from the first canary call at 13:54 to the last
   verdict at 01:00, one cell at a time.
6. **Blinding was partial**, as expected: the instruments saw opaque labels, and no overview named
   its model; the refuters re-derived every scope independently rather than trusting the memo.
7. **The budget rule was changed mid-run**, by the owner, from 69% to 75% while F-med ran, and
   every cell before the change was run under the first line. The change bought the three
   replicates and nothing else.
8. **The copy carried the owner's writing** (6,558 summaries, 8 notes, 111 tag rows, 21 tag
   names), unstripped as in every earlier run; E2 was obeyed in all twelve runs (no run selected
   either column), and the scorers checked the logs for it.

## 6. What the next run adds

A second question is the first thing (design §5). After it, the O2 rule: the trace table is the
reader-facing rule most often skipped (4 of 12), and the guide could say what the table looks
like (one row per overview number, the memo line, the query) the way §15.2 shows the checker
rather than describing it. And a third instrument is not needed: the refuters found nothing in
twelve runs, which says the block's §8 check is being done by the agents themselves (every run
verified its own quotations before finishing), not that refuting is pointless.
